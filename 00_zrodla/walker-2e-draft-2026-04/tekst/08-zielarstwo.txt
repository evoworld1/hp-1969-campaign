Herbology
Herbology is the study of all 
plants and fungi, including 
magical varieties. Witches and 
wizards who study this field 
are known as Herbologists. 
They explore the growth 
patterns of magical plants, 
their preferred habitats, 
cultivation techniques, and the 
crossbreeding of different 
plants. They also study the 
application of these plants in 
potion-making. 
Herbology is taught at 
Hogwarts School of Witchcraft 
and Wizardry. Students learn 
how to properly care for a 
wide variety of plants, as well 
as learning about their useful 
applications and magical 
properties. Hogwarts has 
seven greenhouses that 
students graduate through. 
By the sixth and seventh years 
they will deal with the most 
dangerous and aggressive 
magical plants like Aconite 
and Venomous Tentacula. 
The identification of plants, 
their cultivation, and the 
harvesting of useful 
ingredients can be realised 
through a successful 
1D+Knowledge+Herbology
roll (see below). 
 
Equipment 
Dragon-hide gloves protect 
against burns, bites, and 
corrosive sap, while earmuffs 
block the deadly cries of the 
Mandrake. Students also use 
trowels and pruning shears for 
cultivation, alongside aprons, 
goggles, and enchanted pots 
that regulate temperature for 
sensitive species. 
Useful spells (see pages 47–59) 
for Herbologists include; 
Aguamenti to water plants, the 
Atmospheric Charm to provide 
ideal environments, and 
Defodio to dig channels for 
planting seeds. 
Greenhouses 
There are seven Hogwarts’ 
greenhouses of increasing skill 
and complexity. The most 
challenging and dangerous 
plants are housed in the 
domed greenhouse. 
 
Advanced 
Herbology 
Plants can have their cuttings 
hybridises to combine some of 
their traits (target 21+), like 
combining Fluxweed and 
Knotgrass to create a single 
plant that enhances the 
Polyjuice potion, or mutate a 
plant to gain the abilities of a 
standard spell (target 25+) as 
listed on pages 47–59. 
Advanced Herbology can take 
months of exacting work. 
Typically a number of months 
equal to 12 minus your 
Herbology skill. 
Difficulty Target
Elementary: Identifying a common plant like Dittany or 
Gurdyroot, mixing Dragon Dung compost for seedlings. 3+
Straightforward: Repotting young Flitterblooms, handling 
small Mimbulus mimbletonia cuttings for study. 6+
Tricky: Harvesting Bubotuber pus without skin burns, brewing 
a mild restorative using Shrivelfig and Gurdyroot. 12+
Challenging: Cultivating Fluxweed to maturity precisely at the 
full moon, handling adolescent Mandrakes nearing maturity. 15+
Ambitious: Quelling an overgrown Devil’s Snare infestation 
underground, crossbreeding Flitterbloom and Shrivelfig.
18+
Formidable: Handling a full-grown Venomous Tentacula safely, 
reviving a damaged Whomping Willow with restorative spells. 21+
Almost Impossible: Creating a stable hybrid of Devil’s Snare 
resistant to light, developing a new strain of Dittany with 
enhanced restorative power.

First Year 
Basic magical plants. sSafe 
with supervision 
Asphodel 
A pale lily–like plant used in 
potion brewing. Famously 
combined with wormwood to 
form a potion associated with 
deep regret. Introduced early 
as a common potion 
ingredient and dangerous only 
if misused. 
Knotgrass 
A common magical herb used 
as an ingredient in the 
Polyjuice Potion. Studied 
early, although its more 
advanced uses come later. 
Leaping Toadstool 
A magical fungus that moves 
slightly on its own and is used 
in potion-making. The fungus 
has no hostile traits. 
Sneezewort 
A magical plant that causes 
sneezing and is used in certain 
potions and remedies. 
Causes irritation, but no 
lasting harm. 
Wormwood 
A bitter plant used in potions, 
famously paired with asphodel 
in a draught with symbolic 
associations. Poisonous in 
quantity, but commonly 
handled in early potion and 
herb studies. 
Second Year 
Plants with specific handling 
requirements 
Baneberry 
A toxic magical plant whose 
berries are used in certain 
potions and are dangerous if 
mishandled. Requires gloves 
and careful preparation. 
Fluxweed 
A plant used in the Polyjuice 
Potion, effective only when 
harvested at the full moon, 
which introduces complexity. 
Danger comes from misuse, 
not the plant itself. 
Hellebore 
A toxic, but medicinal, plant 
which requires careful 
handling. Primarily valued for 
its extract, Syrup of Hellebore, 
a key ingredient in calming 
potions like the Draught of 
Peace. 
Shrivelfig 
A magical fruit used in potion 
making that shrivels when 
picked and must be 
rehydrated before use. 
Teaches preservation and 
rehydration techniques. 
The plant has no toxic or 
aggressive properties. 
Third Year 
Plants with strong effects, but 
limited aggression 
Alihotsy 
A powerful magical herb 
whose leaves induce 
uncontrollable laughter when 
consumed. Because of its 
Psychotropic effect, it requires 
careful handling. 
Dittany 
A powerful healing herb that 
causes fresh skin to grow over 
wounds immediately when 
applied. Appropriate for study 
once the basics are mastered. 
Puffapod 
A magical plant that produces 
large pink seed pods, which 
burst into flowers when 
mature. The explosive pods 
add risk, but are predictable 
and non–lethal.

Fourth Year 
Students are now trusted with 
hazardous specimens. 
Chinese Chomping 
Cabbage 
An aggressive magical cabbage 
with snapping jaws, often used 
as a weapon in magical plant 
combat. Handling requires 
quick reflexes and protective 
gear. 
Devil’s Snare 
A sentient and potentially 
lethal, vine–like plant that 
constricts and suffocates 
anything that struggles against 
it, but relaxes in bright light. 
Studied theoretically or under 
tight supervision. 
Mandrake, immature 
A magical plant whose root 
resembles a human and whose 
cry is hazardous when 
unpotted. The shrill cry of an 
immature Mandrake will 
knock out anybody who hears 
it for several hours, but they 
are safe to handle with ear 
protection. The scream of a 
mature Mandrake is powerful 
enough to break glass and will 
kill anybody unlucky enough 
to hear it. Stewed Mandrake is 
an essential part of most 
antidotes including the 
Mandrake Restorative Draught 
which returns those who have 
been petrified back to their 
rightful state. Their leaves are 
also used in potions, including 
the potion needed for the 
Amato Animo Animato 
Animagus spell (see page @@). 
Fifth Year 
(O.W.L. Level) 
Plants with severe 
consequences if mishandled. 
Gillyweed 
A magical plant native to the 
Mediterranean Sea. Eating 
Gillyweed allows a human to 
survive underwater, with 
functioning gills and webbing 
between the fingers and toes. 
Accidental ingestion or over–
exposure to the sap can cause 
the victim to suffocate on dry 
land. Best to keep a bucket of 
water on hand. 
Mandrake, mature 
Because of it lethal cry, it is 
harvested only by advanced 
students and staff (see 
Mandrake, immature) 
Mimbulus Mimbletonia 
A rare magical plant that 
resembles a cactus and 
secretes Stinksap, a foul–
smelling defensive substance. 
Because of its defensive 
secretion, it requires careful 
handling and supervision. 
Sixth–Seventh Year 
(N.E.W.T. Level) 
Legally regulated, highly 
dangerous plants. 
Aconite 
Also known as Monkshood 
and Wolfsbane. An extremely 
poisonous plant used in 
potion making, most notably 
as a key ingredient in the 
Wolfsbane Potion (see page 
@@) that relieves the 
symptoms of lycanthropy. 
Venomous Tentacula 
An aggressive, venomous, and 
toothsome magical plant with 
spiky vines and venomous 
barbs. The leaves are a 
valuable potion ingredient and 
worth ten galleons a piece. 
Venomous Tentacula seeds are 
classified as a Class C non–
tradable substance by the 
Ministry of Magic. 
Bubotuber 
A thick–skinned magical plant 
resembling a slug. 
The bubotuber is filled with 
pus that can be harvested and 
diluted to treat severe acne. 
Extraction of the hazardous 
pus requires protective 
equipment and practiced 
techniques.
