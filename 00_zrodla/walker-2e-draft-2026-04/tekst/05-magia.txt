Spells 
"There was a lot more to magic, 
as Harry quickly found out, than 
waving your wand and saying a 
few funny words." 
Aspell is a controlled 
burst of magic that 
affects the world in 
a supernatural fashion. 
Performing a spell is referred 
to as casting or spellwork 
In the world of Harry Potter, 
once you’ve mastered a spell 
you can cast it with very little 
effort and as many times as 
you wish. The attainment of 
certain spells may take much 
time and effort, but once 
mastered they become second 
nature. Sounds easy doesn’t it? 
Well it’s not. Magic in the 
classroom is one thing, but 
faced with another witch or 
wizard also armed with a wand 
and a toolbox of deadly 
incantations is a much more 
hair–raising prospect. Spells 
need to be used swiftly and 
strategically. It’s not the ability 
to use magic that saves the day, 
but knowing which magic to 
use and the precise moment to 
unleash it. During the Battle 
of Hogwarts, Hermione 
Grainger cast the Hardening 
Charm on a tapestry while 
being pursued by Death 
Eaters. They ran into the stone 
tapestry and were knocked out 
cold, quickly ending their 
pursuit. If Hermione had 
engaged them in combat she 
might have been overpowered 
and captured. It wasn’t the 
spell that saved her, it was her 
strategic thinking. 
Spell Types 
Take a look at the spells listed 
on pages 47–59. Every spell 
has a Type (Charm, Curse, 
Hex, Jinx, Transfiguration, or 
Transportation) and each type 
of magic has an associated 
skill (see table). 
Charm 
A spell that adds properties to 
an object or being temporarily. 
Charms are the most common 
type of spell. 
Curse 
The strongest and most 
wicked of the Dark Charms 
used for the worst kind of 
Dark Magic. 
Hex 
Less severe Dark Charms. 
Hexes are less wicked than 
Curses, but still harmful. 
Jinx 
The least wicked of the Dark 
Charms. Jinxes tend to be 
irritating rather than harmful. 
Transfiguration 
Spells that alter the form or 
appearance of an object or 
being. Where Charms change 
what something does, 
Transfiguration changes what 
something is. 
Transportation 
Spells that move an object or 
being between two points 
almost instantly. Ranging from 
the creation of Portkeys and 
Vanishing Cabinets to the 
enchantments that underly the 
Floo Network. 
Each spell also has a 
Difficulty target for learning 
and casting the spell (see 
opposite). 
Some spells also list a Need. 
This can be a prior spell (e.g. 
knowing the unlocking Charm 
Bombarda before learning the 
more powerful Bombarda 
Maxima), a skill requirement, a 
ritual, a potion, etc. 
There is a magic sheet at the 
end of these rules to record 
your magical attainment and 
to act as a handy spell 
reference.

Spell Difficulty 
Every spell has a starting 
difficulty between 9 and 21. 
This is the target you need to 
roll to learn or cast the spell. 
A simple first–year spell like 
Wingardium Leviosa, the 
Levitation Charm, has a 
difficulty of 9. Whereas 
Protego Horribilis, the most 
powerful shield Charm, has a 
difficulty of 21. 
As you become more familiar 
with a spell, the difficulty 
reduces until it eventually 
reaches zero and you no 
longer need to roll to cast the 
spell. Success becomes 
automatic unless challenging 
circumstances add to the 
difficulty. For example, casting 
a spell without a wand or with 
a wand actively resisting you 
will add to your target and 
require a roll to succeed. 
Learning Magic 
Take a look at the Spell Sheet. 
Each spell has one, two or 
three numbers in boxes next 
to it. For example, these are 
the first four Charms listed: 
Accio (the summoning charm), 
Alarte Ascendare (the rocket 
charm), and Alohomora (the 
unlocking charm) all have a 
starting difficulty of 12. The 
Age Line Spell has a starting 
difficulty of 15. To cast a spell 
you need to roll the relevant 
skill (in this case, Charms). 
You decide to try casting Accio 
and successfully roll 12+. 
You have begun to master the 
Spell and can now fill in the 
first box. The difficulty has 
now dropped to 9. 
The second time you cast the 
spell you need to roll 9 or 
higher to succeed. 
If you successfully roll 9+ you 
can cross off the second box 
and the spell’s difficulty is now 
zero. You’ve mastered the spell. 
So, what’s to stop you working 
your way through the magic 
sheet and learning every spells 
in a week or two? 
Firstly, some spells will be 
beyond you. If you’re a green 
first–year student with a Focus 
Attribute of 1 and no Charms 
skill, then rolling a 12 is 
impossible. 
Secondly, you need to find out 
how to cast the spell. You’ll 
need a book to consult or 
another person to teach you. 
You’ll also need to spend time 
studying the spell before you 
can attempt a roll. 
Take a look at the rules on 
Learning (pages 31–32) for 
details about the different 
routes you can take to learn 
spells. Learning in the 
classroom with a Charms 
expert like Professor Filius 
Flitwick, or with the help of a 
talented fellow student like 
Hermione, will smooth your 
path in a way that no dusty 
spell book ever can. Learning 
can also occur surprisingly 
suddenly when your life 
depends on it (see Learning on 
the Hoof, page 32).

Casting Magic 
If you’ve mastered a spell (see 
page 40) success is automatic, 
but if you haven’t you’ll need 
to roll to cast. Roll 1D10 + 
Focus + the relevant magical 
skill (Charms, Transfiguration, 
etc.) based on the type of spell. 
Add any wand bonuses (see 
pages 27–29). Your target to 
roll is the current difficulty 
for the spell plus any penalties 
that apply: 
Compare the total rolled to 
your target: 
For aimed spells in combat, 
even if you’ve mastered the 
spell you still need to roll to 
hit and instead of using your 
Focus you rely on your Wits. 
See pages 41 for details. 
Duelling 
Duelling is a formal battle 
between two wizards. Duelling 
can be a matter of sport or to 
the death. Your magic sheet 
marks aimed offensive spells 
with a dagger † and offensive 
spells that can become locked 
(see below) with a double 
dagger ‡. Protective spells are 
marked with a triangle ∆. 
These are the primary spells 
you will use for duelling. 
Any other spell is equally 
usable during a duel, but may 
require a certain level of 
creativity to employ effectively. 
Each round of duelling begins 
with both combatants rolling 
initiative. Both sides roll 
1D+Speed+Duelling to decide 
who strikes first. Prior to 
rolling initiative, both sides 
should decide the offensive or 
defensive spells they are 
casting with their two actions. 
If a player character is facing a 
non–player character, then the 
Narrator may decide before 
the player declares or choose a 
response based on the most 
dramatic outcome. If two 
players are facing each other 
then both players should write 
their attack/defence on a piece 
of paper and reveal it prior to 
the initiative roll. 
The initiative winner rolls 
1D+Wits+Duelling to attack. 
The Initiative loser rolls to 
dodge or block (see page 34). 
If the attacker rolls equal or 
higher then the spell succeeds 
(the loser may still have a 
chance to resist the attack as 
per the description of the 
spell). 
If the loser of the initiative is 
still able to cast magic, then it 
is their chance to attack and 
for their opponent to dodge 
or block, and resist as above. 
If the initiative loser sustained 
injuries then penalties will 
apply to their attack. 
In some circumstances the 
spell of attacker and defender 
may lock. 
Locked spells 
When both sides cast lockable 
spells or both roll the same 
initiative, then the beams of 
both wizards fuse together. 
This is duelling at its most 
dramatic. Both duellists 
desperately hold onto their 
wands as the streams meet in 
the middle and they battle to 
overpower the other. 
Each round, the locked 
duellists roll 1D+Focus+Skill
(the skill for the spell they are 
using – e.g. Charms, Dark Arts, 
etc.) The casters are locked 
until one player rolls 5+ points 
above the roll of the other. 
When one combatant wins, 
the other receives the full 
force of the winner’s spell.

Animagi 
The process of becoming an 
Animagus is long and arduous, 
and has the potential to go 
horribly wrong. A failed 
attempt can cause gruesome 
half–human half–animal 
mutations that are permanent 
and cannot be fixed. Many 
witches and wizards simply 
feel that their time might 
better be employed in other 
ways, as the skill is of limited 
practical use unless one has a 
great need of disguise or 
concealment. 
You begin the process of 
becoming an Animagus by 
holding the leaf of a Mandrake 
in your mouth for an entire 
month and reciting an 
incantation (Amato Animo 
Animato Animagus) on a daily 
basis while casting the 
Animagus spell (see page 47). 
You would be wise to master 
the spell before beginning the 
process or risk failing to cast it 
and having to start over. 
The leaf is then used for the 
creation of the Animagus 
Potion (see page 61). You must 
drink the potion during a 
lightning storm to cause your 
first transformation. The shape 
of the creature you will 
transform into appears in your 
mind directly before the 
transformation. You feel “a 
fiery pain and an intense 
double heartbeat.” You may 
feel uncomfortable or 
panicked in your new body, 
which may cause you to 
become frenzied and lash out 
at anything or anybody nearby. 
After the ritual is over, an 
Animagus can then change at 
will without the use of a wand 
or incantation. 
The witch or wizard defines 
the abilities of their animal 
form by redistributing their 
attribute points. Frequently 
transferring their human 
Knowledge and Focus to their 
beast attributes. For example, 
Peter Petigrew transformed 
into a rat and transferred 
some of his knowledge and all 
of his Focus to his Speed and 
Wits. James Potter became a 
stag and transferred 
Knowledge and Focus to his 
Vitality, Speed, and his 
unarmed attack damage 
(antlers). Sirius Black’s 
transference of points from 
Knowledge and Focus to his 
other attributes helped him 
escape from the wizard prison 
of Azkaban. As he explained; 
“Dementors can't see, you 
know...” He swallowed. “They 
feel their way toward people 
by feeding off their 
emotions...They could tell that 
my feelings were less – less 
human, less complex when I 
was a dog.” 
Once the attributes for your 
animal form are decided they 
are fixed and you cannot shift 
points around any further. 
All Animagi are required to 
register with the Ministry of 
Magic at the Animagus 
Registry which is a publicly 
available record. 
Resisting Magic 
"Some charms will be ineffective 
on large creatures such as trolls, 
whose hides repel all but the 
more powerful spells." 
Miranda Goshawk, 
The Standard Book of Spells, 
Grade 1 
Most wizards, Half–Giant’s 
aside, lack any immunity 
against magic spells. Shield 
Hats, Shield Cloaks, and 
Shield Gloves, enchanted by 
Fred and George Weasley, are 
widely used by Ministry of 
Magic officials. These 
enchanted items protect the 
wearer from light to moderate 
curses, hexes, jinxes, and other 
unfriendly spells, but are 
useless against more powerful 
magic, including the 
Unforgivable Curses. 
Of the three Unforgivable 
Curses, only the Imperius 
Curse can be resisted, but that 
requires great strength of will. 
Wizards can also practice the 
obscure art of Occlumency to 
protect their mind from 
external manipulation or 
penetration. 
The ultimate protection is the 
one that saved young Harry 
Potter from Lord Voldemort’s 
Killing Curse. Sacrificial 
protection, an ancient, 
powerful, and long–lasting 
counter–charm, is endowed 
when a person sacrifices their 
own life willingly and out of 
deep, pure love to save the life 
of one or more people.

Shield clothing 
Shield hats, cloaks, and gloves 
are enchanted objects (see 
page 71) imbued with the 
qualities of Bestowal and the 
standard Shield spell. Shield 
Hats cost 10 Galleons each, 
but were sold to the Ministry 
of Magic for 5 Galleons a 
piece. Shield Gloves cost 8
Galleons, and Shield Cloaks 
cost 20 Galleons. From 1996, 
these are available from 
Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes at 
No. 93 Diagon Alley.

Imperius Curse 
If you attempt to resist the 
curse, roll 1D10+Focus 
+D.A.D.A (Defence against 
the Dark Arts). The person 
attempting to control you 
must roll 1D10+Focus 
+Dark Arts. The resister ca 
also use their Occlumency 
skill instead of D.A.D.A. 
Plainly, Barty Crouch Jnr. 
rolled a 1 or 2 when he tried 
to use the Imperius curse on 
Harry Potter, or Harry used a 
Story Point to roll and extra 
dice (see page 4). 
Occlumency 
The magical discipline that 
protects the mind against 
external manipulation or 
penetration, The basic method 
involves emptying the mind, 
but advanced practitioners can 
selectively hide specific 
thoughts, emotions, or 
memories. The art of trying to 
penetrate another’s mind is 
Legilimency (see page 53). 
The witch or wizard 
attempting to penetrate the 
targets mind (the Legilimens) 
rolls 1D10+Focus+Charms. 
The target attempting to 
protect themselves with 
Occlumency (the Occlumens) 
rolls 1D10+Focus+ 
Occlumency. Compare their 
rolls as below. 
Sacrificial Protection 
For sacrificial protection to be 
invoked, the killer must give 
the victim the option to live, 
but the victim must 
consciously and willingly 
choose death. Lily Potter was 
offered the chance to step 
aside because Voldemort had 
promised Severus Snape that 
he wouldn't kill her unless she 
got in his way. Her conscious 
refusal to comply with 
Voldemort's demand was why 
unusually strong magical 
protection was conferred upon 
her son. 
After the sacrifice, the 
protection lives on in the 
beneficiary's veins. The 
protection prevents whoever 
had murdered the victim from 
physically touching the 
beneficiary without 
experiencing excruciating 
pain, and causes a Killing 
Curse cast at the beneficiary 
by the murderer to rebound. 
Sacrificial Protection can 
effect a group of people, but 
the protection is diluted, at 
the discretion of the Narrator.

The Patronus Spell 
A Patronus is a kind of Anti–
Dementor (see page 93). 
A guardian that acts as a 
shield between you and 
Dementors. A Patronus is a 
positive force, a projection of 
the very things that the 
Dementor feeds upon, hope, 
happiness, and the desire to 
survive, but a Patronus cannot 
feel despair, so Dementors 
can’t harm it. A Patronus has 
two forms; non–corporeal and 
corporeal. A non–corporeal 
Patronus appears as thin wisps 
of silver that hover like mist, 
but a corporeal Patronus has a 
form that is clearly defined 
and is more than vapour or 
smoke. The Patronus Charm, 
Expecto Patronum (see page 51) 
is a very challenging Charm 
and not all witches and 
wizards can summon a 
corporeal Patronus, never 
mind successfully casting the 
spell in the terrifying presence 
of a Dementor (see Fear, page 
89). Those that can produce a 
full Patronus may choose to 
produce a non–corporeal 
Patronus in order to conceal 
their identity. 
Corporeal Patronuses take the 
form of whatever animal the 
caster has the greatest affinity 
for. Dogs, cats, and horses are 
among the most common 
Patronuses, given those 
animals' long rapport with 
humans. Every Patronus is as 
unique as its creator, and even 
identical twins have been 
known to produce very 
different Patronuses. 
If a witch or wizard is an 
Animagus and can summon a 
corporeal Patronus, their 
animal form and their 
Patronus animal will 
frequently be the same. 
To successfully cast the spell 
you must summon your 
happiest memory. The happier 
the memory, the better the 
charm will work. The next step 
is to begin drawing circles 
with your wand in the air to 
increase the power of the 
spell, and then call out the 
incantation; Expecto Patronum. 
If successful, the Patronus 
emerges from the tip of your 
wand and can be directed 
towards a target. Patronuses 
can be directed verbally and 
will take instructions from the 
caster. 
It’s possible, in some cases. 
for a Patronus to change form 
in response to a fundamental 
emotional change. This can 
happen when a witch or 
wizard falls in deep and 
eternal love. Because 
Patronuses are conjured by 
recalling happy memories, 
when those memories revolve 
around a person the caster is 
truly in love with, their 
Patronus may change to a 
form complementary to the 
Patronus of their loved one. 
It is generally believed that 
only those of pure heart are 
able to cast the Patronus 
Charm. This is not entirely 
true. Dark witches and wizards 
generally don’t try to produce 
a Patronus, having no need for 
one. Being desensitised to the 
effects of the dark creatures 
they ally themselves with or 
have under their control. 
However, some witches and 
wizards of questionable 
morals, like Dolores 
Umbridge, are still able to 
produce corporeal–Patronuses. 
This may be because they have 
a true and confident, although 
erroneous, belief in their own 
righteousness. 
All player characters can
produce a Patronus if they
choose to learn the Expecto
Patronum Charm (player are,
after all, exceptional witches
and wizards), but their
Patronus will only become
fully corporeal when they
have mastered the spell and
filled in all three boxes on
their spell sheet (see page 40).
