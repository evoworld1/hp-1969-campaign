The Wizarding British Isles
Hidden from Muggle* 
eyes, a thriving 
society of witches and 
wizards live across the British 
Isles. Beneath Whitehall in 
London, a sprawling complex 
houses the Ministry of Magic. 
A vast bureaucracy that 
monitors and regulates magical 
activity. 
Less than half a mile from the 
Ministry is Diagon Alley (see 
page 142). A hidden shopping 
district accessed through the 
Leaky Cauldron pub on 
Charing Cross Road. Along 
Diagon Alley, witches and 
wizards can buy wands, 
cauldrons, potion ingredients , 
and much much more. The 
imposing marble frontage of 
Gringotts Wizarding Bank 
dominates the street, with 
goblin–run vaults stretching 
deep beneath the city. Tucked 
away nearby is Knockturn 
Alley (see page 144), a more 
sinister thoroughfare where 
traders steeped in dark magic 
attract a discreet clientele. 
About a mile and a half North 
from Diagon Alley is King’s 
Cross Station where young 
witches and wizards, travelling 
to Britain’s wizarding school 
Hogwarts (see pages 151–161), 
catch the scarlet Hogwart 
Express from Platform Nine 
and Three-Quarters. The train 
takes them on a 400 mile 
journey to Hogsmeade, the 
only entirely non-Muggle 
settlement in Britain. 
From Hogsmeade station, 
students travel to Hogwarts. 
An ancient castle in the 
Scottish Highlands where 
young witches and wizards 
have been taught magic for 
nearly a millennium. Hogwarts 
School of Witchcraft and 
Wizardry is both a sanctuary 
and a crucible of magical life. 
Its four Houses; Gryffindor, 
Slytherin, Ravenclaw, and 
Hufflepuff, have shaped 
generations of British wizards. 
Wizard homes are scattered 
across the country. The grand 
houses of old pureblood 
families, like No. 12 
Grimmauld place in London, 
home of the Black family, and 
Malfoy Manor in Wiltshire. 
More humble dwellings like 
The Burrow in Devon near the 
village of Ottery St Catchpole, 
the welcoming home of the 
Weasley clan, Shell Cottage 
overlooking the Cornish coast, 
home of Bill Weasley and Fleur 
Delacour, the Lovegood House, 
also near Ottery St Catchpole, 
the dwelling of Xenophilius 
Lovegood and his daughter 
Luna, and Godric’s Hollow, 
home of the Dumbledore 
family and birthplace of both 
Harry Potter and Godric 
Gryffindor. Following his 
parents’ murder, Harry would 
grow up in the very un–
magical 4 Privet Drive in Little 
Whinging, Surrey, only to have 
his heritage revealed in the 
lonely Hut–on–the–rock. 
 
There are also more Sinister 
sites. Little Hangleton, home of 
the violent and unstable Gaunt 
family who would sire Tom 
Riddle, later to become Lord 
Voldemort, and the scene for 
many other dark and horrible 
events, and the lonely 
windswept coast overlooking 
Azkaban, the infamous wizard 
prison guarded by Dementors 
who feed on the misery of the 
prison’s inmates (see page 93). 
Explore some of these 
legendary locations in the 
following pages and the map 
opposite.
* non–magical folk.

Wizarding London
London locations: 
1. London Zoo
The world's oldest 
scientific zoo, located in 
Regent's Park in London. 
Unknown to the Muggle 
population, it also has a 
hidden wing dedicated to 
magical creatures. Many 
incorrectly believe that 
London Zoo is where 
Harry Potter first found 
that he could speak 
Parseltongue, but that was 
at an unnamed Surrey zoo. 
2. Kings Cross Station
One of the main London 
train stations. Young 
witches and wizards take 
the Hogwarts Express from 
Platform 9¾ every 1st
September at 11 a.m. 
3. 12 Grimmauld Place
The ancestral home of the 
Black family, located in the 
Borough of Islington. 
The house is protected by 
“every security measure 
known to wizardkind”. 
The house is invisible to 
Muggles who have long 
since accepted that 
number thirteen is next 
door to number eleven. 
4. St Mungo's Hospital for
Magical Maladies and
Injuries
A hospital serving the 
wizarding population of 
Great Britain. The building 
is disguised as a 
department store closed 
for renovations. 
5. The Leaky Cauldron
A wizard pub hidden from 
Muggle eyes and the 
entrance to Diagon Alley 
(see page 142), and 
indirectly, Knockturn Alley 
(see page 144). 
6. The Ministry of Magic
The governing body of the 
magical community of 
Great Britain and Ireland. 
Enforcing wizarding law 
and maintaining the 
statute of secrecy. The 
headquarters is under the 
HM Treasury building and 
spans Whitehall. 
7. St Cyprian Orphanage
Childhood home of Tom 
Riddle. The future dark 
Lord Voldemort.

Diagon Alley
Diagon Alley is a 
cobblestone shopping 
district located in 
London behind the Leaky 
Cauldron, a pub on the 
Charing Cross Road. 
To Muggles the pub looks like 
a shabby broken–down 
shopfront between a 
bookshop and a record shop, 
but beyond it’s rear courtyard 
lie an assortment of wizarding 
businesses that can supply all 
the equipment required by a 
Hogwarts student and more 
besides. The alley is extensive, 
but is completely hidden 
following the imposition of 
the Statute of Secrecy. Diagon 
Alley is very much the centre 
of wizarding London and 
there are a number of 
locations for player to explore. 
Amanuensis Quills 
A specialist in luxury quills 
and exotic inks. 
Cranville Quincey’s
Magical Junkshop 
A tiny junk shop on South 
Side selling broken wands, 
lopsided brass scales, and old 
cloaks covered in potion 
stains. 
Broomstix 
A place to buy everyday 
broomsticks new or 
secondhand, and to bring your 
current model for repairs, 
retwigging, and retuning. If 
you want something more 
sporty you’ll need to go to 
Quality Quidditch Supplies 
further along North Side. 
Eeylops Owl Emporium 
A shop on North Side that 
sells owls and owl supplies, 
including owl nuts and their 
own mouse shaped Eeylops 
Premium Owl Treats. They sell 
Barn, Brown, and Tawny owls 
for 10 Galleons, Barred owls 
for 11 Galleons, and Screech 
and Snowy owls for 15
Galleons. Eeylops also have 
branches in Glasgow and 
Leeds. 
Florean Fortescue’s Ice
Cream Parlour 
A small shop on the North 
Side next to the secondhand 
bookshop. The tables outside 
are a pleasant place to take a 
break from shopping and 
enjoy one of Florean’s Ices.

Diagon Alley
Flourish and Blotts
Bookseller 
Located half–way down North 
Side, Flourish and Blotts is 
where most Hogwarts students 
buy their schoolbooks (mostly 
priced at 1 to 2 Galleons). The 
shop is lined with shelves of 
books from floor to ceiling as 
well as books displayed on 
tables and piled strategically 
here and there on the floor. 
Gambol and Japes Wizarding
Joke Shop 
Located on the South Side. 
The place to go for your 
Snapping Teacups, 
Dungbombs, Frog Spawn 
Soaps, Non–explodable 
Luminous Balloons, and Dr 
Filibuster’s Fabulous Wet–
start No–heat Fireworks. 
Gringotts Wizarding Bank 
The only bank in the 
wizarding world. Where the 
wealth of British witches and 
wizards sits in heavily guarded 
vaults deep below ground. 
Gringotts (est. 1474) is 
operated solely by Goblins 
under the motto Fortius Quo 
Fidelius (Strength Through 
Loyalty). The main offices are 
located on North Side in a 
white marble multi–storied 
building that towers over the 
neighbouring shops. Muggle 
money can be exchanged for 
wizard currency at Gringotts. 
Mr Mulpepper’s Apothecary 
Located on the North Side. Its 
fascinating ingredients make 
up for its horrible smell. A 
mixture of bad eggs and rotted 
cabbages.Barrels of slimy stuff 
stood on the floor; jars of 
herbs, dried roots, and bright 
powders lined the walls; 
bundles of feathers, strings of 
fangs, and snarled claws hang 
from the ceiling. You can buy 
anything from Bat Spleens 
and Beetle Eyes to Unicorn 
Horns and Dragon Liver. 
Madam Malkin's Robes for
All Occasions 
Malkin's is a shop on North 
Side where students get their 
Hogwarts school uniforms, 
and wizards and witches can 
purchase dress robes, self–
ironing and self–repairing 
robes, gloves, travelling cloaks, 
and, on very rare occasions, 
invisibility cloaks. 
Magical Menagerie 
A magical creature shop on 
North Side. The shop is 
cramped and noisy with every 
inch of wall covered in cages 
and tanks containing Fire 
Crabs, Rats, Nifflers, Toads, 
Owls, Cats, Pygmy Puffs, and 
much more. 
Obscurus Books 
A wizarding book publisher. 
Their range includes Fantastic 
Beasts and Where to Find Them
by Newt Scamander. 
Ollivanders 
Located on South Side, 
Ollivanders (EST. 382BC) is 
widely acknowledged to be the 
finest wand makers in Great 
Britain. A solitary wand lies on 
a faded purple cushion in the 
window. The dusty interior is 
bare apart from a spindly chair 
in the corner and thousands 
of boxed wands piled from 
floor to ceiling around the 
walls of the shop. Garrick 
Ollivander himself will help 
you select your wand. 
Although it is more accurate 
to say that he will help the 
wand select you. 
Potage’s Cauldron Shop 
The first shop on North Side 
you encounter on entering 
Diagon Alley, Madam Potage’s 
shop sells Pewter, Brass, 
Copper, and Silver cauldrons, 
as well as self–stirring and 
collapsible models. 
Quality Quidditch Supplies 
Everything the budding 
Quidditch player needs. The 
Store stocks racing brooms, 
Quidditch Starter Kits, and all 
manner of equipments, 
including British and Irish 
League strips. 
Scribbulus Writing
Impliments 
Seller of quills, ink (including 
invisible and Colour–change 
ink), parchment, and other 
items of stationery. 
Secondhand Books 
Affordable books and 
antiquarian surprises. 
Secondhand Robes 
A great source of robes for 
less wealthy families who 
don’t mind being a little 
behind the fashions.. 
Slug and Jiggers Apothecary 
A trader in potion ingredients 
similar to Mulpepper’s but 
focussed on the domestic 
market rather than trade. For 
this reason they are favoured 
by Hogwarts students.

Knockturn Alley
Sugarplum’s Sweet Shop 
A popular shop with Hogwarts 
students. Suggarplum’s sells 
Chocolate Frogs, Bertie Bott’s 
Every Flavour Beans, 
Drooble’s Best Blowing Gum, 
Pumpkin Pasties, Cauldron 
Cakes, Liquorice Wands, and 
all sorts of magical treats. 
The Daily Prophet 
By far the most widely read 
wizarding newspaper in 
Britain. The Daily Prophet is 
delivered by owl daily to 
nearly every wizarding 
household in Britain. From its 
head office in Diagon Alley it 
also publishes the Evening 
Prophet and Sunday Prophet. 
The Prophet prefers scandal 
to facts and is often biased 
and factually inaccurate. 
Twillfitt and Tattings 
A high–class Wizarding 
clothes shop on South Side. 
More upmarket than Madam 
Malkin’s and favoured by 
wealthy Pureblood families. 
Wiseacre’s Wizarding
Equipment 
Selling a wide range of 
wizarding supplies from their 
North Side establishment. 
Among other items, Wiseacre’s 
sells; hour glasses, magnifying 
glasses, binoculars, telescopes, 
weighing scales (Silver and 
Brass), compasses, glass and 
crystal phials, crystal balls, a 
range of astronomical charts, 
as well as globes, and 
Armillary Spheres. For a full 
inventory and price list, see 
page 148. 
Knockturn Alley is a 
rather dodgy shopping 
area off Diagon Alley 
that caters to the Dark Arts 
and the criminal fraternity. 
One of the more infamous 
establishments is Borgin and
Burke’s, an antique shop 
known to trade in unusual and 
malevolent artefacts like the 
Hand of a Glory, hangman’s 
rope, and cursed jewllery. 
Other notable traders include; 
Coffin House that sells books 
and paraphernalia relating to 
the Dark Art of Necromancy, 
the raising of the dead, and 
communicating with the other 
side. Dystyl Phaelanges that 
trade in bones, fossils and 
fangs, as well as pieces of 
furniture crafted from bones. 
Noggin and Bonce who 
specialise in shrunken heads. 
Tallow and Hemp Toxic
Tapers who sell their own 
handmade poisonous candles. 
Trackleshanks Locksmiths
who sound honest enough, 
but it’s hard to imagine, given 
their location, that they don’t 
serve those who want to 
bypass locks as much as they 
serve those that seek security. 
Shyverwretch’s Venoms and
Poisons who’s window display 
of large venomous spiders very 
much illustrates their stock in 
trade, ranging from common 
poisons to Basilisk venom. 
It is recommended that 
Hogwarts students stay clear 
of Knockturn Alley. Many a 
lost innocent has failed to 
emerge from that sinister 
byway and never been seen 
again.

Hogsmeade is the only 
all–wizarding village in 
the British Isles. An 
attractive hamlet of cottages, 
shops, and eccentrically 
ordered gardens, where 
enchanted candles festoon the 
trees during the holidays. 
Hogsmeade is very near the 
train station used by the 
Hogwarts Express from Kings 
Cross Station (see page 141). 
Students arrive at the tiny 
platform located by Hogwarts’ 
Black Lake (see page 152). 
first–year students travel 
across the lake by boat 
accompanied by Professor 
Hagrid (see page 118), but 
later years are transported by 
carriages drawn by Thestrals 
(see page 101). 
Third–Year Hogwarts 
students and older are 
allowed to visit Hogsmeade 
during weekends, with 
parental permission. 
Shops on Hogsmeade High 
Street include; Ceridwen's 
Cauldrons, Scrivenshaft's 
Quill Shop, Pippin’s Potions, 
Zonko's Joke Shop, 
Honeydukes sweet shop, 
Dogweed and Deathcap who 
trade in exotic plants, and 
flowers, Gladrags Wizardwear 
who specialise in strange and 
unusual socks, the Hogsmeade 
Post Office housing at least 
two or three hundred owls, 
and near the end of the High 
Street, Dervish and Banges, a 
shop selling and repairing 
magical equipment and 
stocking novelties like 
Sneakoscopes and toy brooms. 
Two Diagon Alley favourites 
also have branches in 
Hogsmeade; Potage's 
Cauldron Shop and 
Ollivander’s, makers of fine 
wands. 
There are two pubs in 
Hogsmeade. The popular 
Three Broomsticks Inn, 
managed by Madam Rosmerta, 
and the more disreputable 
Hog’s Head, owned and 
operated by Aberforth 
Dumbledore. 
The Three Broomsticks 
is warm, crowded, and 
a bit smoky, but clean 
and welcoming. 
 
 
Hogwarts students go to the 
Three Broomsticks for 
Butterbeer while school staff 
and other residents enjoy 
Firewhisky, Mulled Mead, and 
Red Currant Rum. Cheaper, 
but less respectable, is the 
Hog’s Head Inn located one or 
two streets back from the High 
Street. If you’re looking for 
somewhere more genteel, 
Madam Puddifoot's Tea Shop, 
popular with dating teenagers, 
can be found on a side road 
off the High Street. 
The Wizarding Wireless 
Network broadcasts news 
and light entertainment 
from Hogsmeade. 
A hugely popular 
wireless station, listened 
to by most wizarding 
homes.

Hogwarts School
Every British child who is 
invited to attend Hogwarts 
receives a letter of acceptance 
written in green ink on 
parchment. delivered to the 
recipient by owl, except in the 
case of Muggleborns. When a 
student is Muggleborn the 
acceptance letter is delivered 
in person by a member of 
school staff who explains 
everything to the student and 
their parents or guardian. 
The letter reads: 
HOGWARTS SCHOOL of 
WITCHCRAFT and 
WIZARDRY.

Hogwarts School
The journey to Hogwarts 
School marks a profound 
transition in the lives of its 
students, particularly for first–
years. Their arrival is carefully 
planned to inspire awe, 
encourage belonging, and 
impress on them the traditions 
they are now part of. 
The journey from King’s Cross 
Station in London to 
Hogsmeade in Scotland takes 
nearly nine hours. Tentative 
friendships often begin on this 
long journey. Most students 
are excited to be travelling to 
the school they have heard so 
much about from their parents 
or older siblings, but there is 
also trepidation, particularly 
among Muggleborn students 
who have only recently 
entered the wizarding world. 
When students finally arrive at 
Hogsmeade Station it is dusk. 
Older students travel to the 
castle by carriage. First–years 
are guided by Hagrid, Keeper 
of Keys and Grounds, to the 
edge of the Black Lake. There 
they board small boats that 
glide silently across the water. 
Ahead of them is the dark 
outline of the castle glittering 
with pinpricks of light. They 
clamber out at the boathouse 
and climb the zig–zagging 
stone steps leading up to the 
Chamber of Reception. New 
students are greeted by the 
Deputy Headmaster or 
Headmistress. Between 1956
and 1997, Professor Minerva 
McGonagal, Head of 
Gryffindor House, holds this 
role. Students wait nervously 
to enter the castle. 
Finally, the first–years are led 
through the enormous oak 
doors and into the Entrance 
Hall. The high ceilings, 
shifting staircases, and the 
distant glow of torchlight all 
signal that Hogwarts is both 
ancient and alive. First-years 
are then ushered into the 
Great Hall, where the rest of 
the school waits beneath 
floating candles and a 
bewitched ceiling that mirrors 
the night sky. 
New students, particularly 
those with older siblings at the 
school, know that they will be 
sorted into houses, but they 
probably have a hazy notion 
about the exact mechanism 
and may be surprised to see a 
tatty old pointed hat sitting on 
a stool. They will be further 
surprised to find that this 
battered hat, the Sorting Hat, 
will decide their path at 
Hogwarts and even the shape 
of their future lives. The hat 
introduces itself with a song, 
a new one every year. One by 
one, students are called 
forward to don the venerable 
hat, which looks deep into 
their mind to determine the 
house (see pages 153–154) that 
best suits their character, their 
gifts, and their ambition; 
Gryffindor, Hufflepuff, 
Ravenclaw, or Slytherin. Some 
are declared almost instantly, 
others take a little longer. On 
the rare occasion that the 
Sorting Hat struggles to 
choose between two houses, 
sometimes for 
five minutes or 
more. This is 
known as a hatstall. 
Cheers erupt as each house is 
declared, and anxiety gives 
way to relief and pride. 
Students are welcomed at 
their house tables. Greeted by 
Prefects, older pupils, and 
their Head of House. Amid 
feasting and celebration, new 
students find themselves not 
just admitted to a school, but 
embraced by a house that will 
become their family within the 
hoary castle walls of Hogwarts. 
After the feast, students are 
dismissed from the Great Hall 
by their Head of House. 
Prefects and older students 
help guide the first–years to 
their new living quarters; 
The Gryffindor rooms in the 
West tower, Hufflepuff’s 
hidden behind a stack of 
barrels in the basement near 
the kitchens, Ravenclaw in 
their tall tower, and Slytherin 
down in the dungeons. Inside 
the house common room, the 
Head of House gives a brief 
welcome speech and outlines 
some of the rules and 
expectations for the year 
ahead. The exhausted students 
find their dormitories, unpack 
their trunks, and get settled 
in for their first night at 
Hogwarts. Looking 
forward, perhaps 
nervously, to the next 
morning when 
classes officially 
begin (see pages 
155–156).

The Four Hogwarts Houses
Hogwarts was founded by four 
great witches and wizards; 
Godric Gryffindor, Helga 
Hufflepuff, Rowena Ravenclaw, 
and Salazar Slytherin. The 
founders preserved their 
philosophies in the four 
School Houses they stamped 
with their personalities. 
Gryffindor 
Founded by Godric 
Gryffindor, a wizard celebrated 
for his daring and his belief 
that magic should be wielded 
boldly. Godric prized courage 
above all else, particularly the 
moral courage to stand against 
injustice. Gryffindor 
instructed the Sorting Hat to 
only select students that 
possessed the characteristics 
he most valued; courage, 
chivalry, and determination. 
The ethos of Gryffindor is one 
of daring and defiance in the 
face of danger. Gryffindors 
value action over hesitation 
and tend to see the world as 
something to be challenged 
rather than endured. At their 
best, Gryffindors are just, 
heroic, and inspiring. Willing 
to sacrifice comfort and safety 
for others. At their worst, they 
can be impulsive, hot–headed, 
and blind to subtler solutions. 
Gryffindor teaches that fear is 
not the absence of bravery, but 
the fire that bravery feeds 
upon, and that sometimes you 
must face danger head on. 
The Gryffindor common room 
lies high in Gryffindor Tower, 
concealed behind the portrait 
of the Fat Lady on the seventh 
floor. Entry requires a spoken 
password, which changes 
frequently and is delivered 
with operatic flair by the 
portrait’s occupant. Beyond 
the fat lady’s portrait, the 
room is warm and welcoming. 
Full of squashy armchairs, a 
crackling fire, and windows 
with grand views the grounds. 
It is a place that encourages 
late–night debates, loud 
laughter, and ill–advised plans. 
Gryffindor’s house ghost is 
Nearly Headless Nick, formally 
Sir Nicholas de Mimsy–
Porpington. Polite, 
melancholic, and deeply 
sensitive about his incomplete 
beheading, Nick embodies the 
house’s often tragic heroism. 
He is a reminder that courage 
does not always lead to 
triumph, but that dignity can 
survive failure. 
Hufflepuff
Hufflepuff House was founded 
by Helga Hufflepuff, a witch 
known for her generosity and 
practical wisdom. Unlike the 
other founders, Helga believed 
that magical education should 
be open to all who were 
willing to learn. She valued 
patience, loyalty, and hard 
work, and famously took 
students “just and true” 
regardless of pedigree or flash. 
The ethos of Hufflepuff 
centres on fairness, 
perseverance, and quiet 
strength. Hufflepuffs are often 
underestimated, but they 
possess a deep well of 
resilience and believe that 
effort matters more than glory 
and that communities thrive 
when everyone contributes. 
At their best, Hufflepuffs are 
steadfast friends and tireless 
workers. At their worst, they 
may be stubborn, self–effacing, 
or slow to assert themselves 
when decisive action is 
required. 
The Hufflepuff common room 
is located near the Hogwarts 
kitchens, behind a stack of 
barrels in the basement. Entry 
requires tapping a particular 
barrel in a precise rhythm. 
An incorrect attempt results in 
the offender being doused 
with vinegar. The common 
room is low–ceilinged, 
furnished with comfortable 
overstuffed furniture, and 
decorated in cosy earth tones. 
It feels like a welcoming 
burrow, reflecting the house’s 
emphasis on comfort and 
belonging. 
Hufflepuff’s house ghost is the 
Fat Friar, a jovial and absent–
minded apparition clad in 
monastic robes. He is 
endlessly forgiving and 
inclined to apologise for 
things that are not his fault. 
The Friar represents 
Hufflepuff’s compassion and 
humility, as well as its 
tendency to be overlooked 
despite its moral backbone.

Ravenclaw 
Ravenclaw House was founded 
by Rowena Ravenclaw, a witch 
famed for her intelligence and 
creativity. Rowena believed 
that magic flourished best in 
curious minds and that wit 
was as valuable as raw power. 
She sought students who 
delighted in learning for its 
own sake, valuing originality 
and insight above rote mastery. 
The ethos of Ravenclaw is 
intellectual curiosity, wisdom, 
and imagination. Ravenclaws 
prize knowledge, but not 
merely as facts to be collected; 
they seek understanding and 
new ways of seeing the world. 
At their best, they are 
inventive thinkers and 
insightful problem–solvers. 
At their worst, they can be 
aloof, pedantic, or paralysed by 
overthinking. 
The Ravenclaw common room 
sits in a tower on the west side 
of the castle. Unlike other 
houses, it has no password. 
Instead, entry requires 
answering a riddle posed by 
an enchanted bronze eagle 
knocker. This ensures that 
those who enter have engaged 
their minds, though it can lead 
to frustrated students pacing 
the corridor at all hours. The 
common room is airy and 
elegant, filled with books, 
desks, and wide windows that 
offer sweeping views of the 
mountains and sky. 
Ravenclaw’s house ghost is the 
Grey Lady, Helena Ravenclaw, 
daughter of the founder. 
Reserved and sorrowful, she 
embodies the costs of pride 
and the burden of unfulfilled 
ambition. Her presence is a 
quiet counterpoint to the 
house’s celebration of 
intellect, reminding students 
that wisdom must be 
tempered with humility. 
Slytherin 
Slytherin House was founded 
by Salazar Slytherin, a 
brilliant and controversial 
wizard who believed that 
magical education should be 
reserved for those of pure 
magical lineage. While his 
views on blood purity are now 
widely condemned, his house 
endures. 
The ethos of Slytherin 
revolves around ambition, 
cunning, and self–
preservation. Slytherins are 
goal-oriented and resourceful. 
Adept at navigating complex 
social and political landscapes. 
At their best, they are strategic 
leaders who achieve lasting 
change. At their worst, they 
can be manipulative, ruthless, 
or excessively suspicious. 
Slytherin teaches that power is 
not inherently evil, but that 
how it is pursued and used 
defines its worth. 
The Slytherin common room 
lies beneath the Black Lake, 
accessed through a concealed 
entrance in the dungeons 
behind a stretch of bare stone 
wall. A spoken password 
causes the wall to slide open. 
The common room is long and 
low, with green–tinted light 
filtering through underwater 
windows. Its atmosphere is 
cool and austere, designed to 
impress and intimidate in equal 
measure. 
Slytherin’s house ghost is the 
Bloody Baron, a fearsome 
spectre whose chains clatter 
ominously through the 
corridors. He is silent, severe, 
and widely feared, even by 
other ghosts. The Baron 
embodies the house’s darker 
history and its capacity for 
intimidation, serving as a 
reminder that ambition without 
restraint can leave lasting scars. 
The Four Hogwarts Houses
Students can win points for 
their House by doing good 
deeds, correctly answering 
questions in classes, or for 
victories like winning an 
inter–house Quidditch 
match. Points can also be 
taken away for rule–
breaking. Points are 
awarded by Professors and 
Prefects (not always fairly). 
The house with the most 
points at the end of the 
year wins the prestigious 
House Cup.

Hogwarts School
Curriculum 
Hogwarts School of Witchcraft 
and Wizardry offers a broad 
and carefully structured 
magical curriculum designed to 
provide young witches and 
wizards with both practical 
skills and a solid theoretical 
foundation. Students begin 
their education at age eleven 
and follow a common 
programme for their first five 
years, leading to the Ordinary 
Wizarding Levels (O.W.L.s), 
before specialising for their 
final two years in preparation 
for Nastily Exhausting 
Wizarding Tests (N.E.W.T.s). 
Seven core subjects are 
compulsory for all students, as 
well as broomstick flying 
classes which are only 
compulsory during the 
students’ first year. 
Transfiguration, taught for 
many years by Professor 
Minerva McGonagall, focuses 
on the precise and demanding 
art of changing the form and 
nature of objects and creatures. 
Charms, under Professor 
Filius Flitwick, trains students 
in spells that alter an object’s 
behaviour rather than its 
substance. Potions, mercilessly 
overseen by Professor Severus 
Snape, combines magical 
theory with exacting practical 
work, rewarding precision and 
patience. 
Herbology (see page 67), 
taught by Professor Pomona 
Sprout, covers magical plants 
and fungi, many of which are 
dangerous or volatile, while 
Defence Against the Dark 
Arts (D.A.D.A.) equips 
students to protect themselves 
against curses, dark creatures, 
and hostile magic. This subject 
is notorious for its high 
turnover of professors, ranging 
from the inept to the genuinely 
talented, reflecting the dangers 
inherent in the discipline. 
The last two core subjects are 
far more theoretical; History of 
Magic, delivered by the ghost 
of Professor Cuthbert Binns, is 
a famously dry litany of 
significant names, dates and 
events in wizarding history 
delivered in Binns’ soporific 
droning voice, and Astronomy, 
taught by Professor Aurora 
Sinistra, which convenes at the 
top of the Astronomy Tower 
every Wednesday 
at midnight. 
From their third year, students 
may choose electives. Care of 
Magical Creatures (see page 
85), taught by Rubeus Hagrid, 
among others, introduces 
students to the handling and 
understanding of magical 
beasts. Divination, under 
Professor Sybill Trelawney, 
explores the prediction of 
future events, though its 
academic reliability is often 
questioned. Arithmancy
focuses on the magical 
properties of numbers, Ancient 
Runes covers reading and 
interpreting old magical scripts, 
and Muggle Studies explores 
the lives of non–magical folk. 
In the sixth and seventh years, 
students narrow their studies to 
subjects relevant to their 
intended careers, such as Auror 
training or Healing. Advanced 
instruction is more rigorous 
and independent, reflecting 
adult magical responsibilities. 
The Hogwarts curriculum 
blends ancient magical 
philosophies with practical 
skills and insights. Through its 
curriculum and the guidance of 
its professors, Hogwarts 
prepares students not only to 
pass examinations, but to 
survive and thrive in the wider 
wizarding world.

The Hogwarts Day 
Morning (7.30–9am.) 
Students gather for a lavish 
breakfast in the Great Hall. 
Options on every table 
include; corn flakes and icy 
cold milk, steaming porridge, 
eggs, sausage, bacon, warm 
bread rolls and toast (with 
butter, jam, or marmalade), 
and jugs of orange, apple, and 
pumpkin juice. Morning mail 
is delivered by owls. A bell 
rings at 9am, signalling the 
start of the school day when 
students should be ready at 
their desks. Two classes or a 
double–length class are held 
in the morning, followed by a 
short break. 
 
Morning break (11–11.15am) 
Students can socialise and 
explore the nearby castle and 
grounds. They can also visit 
the library or consult books. 
Late Morning (11.15am–
l.15pm) Classes resume after 
break with a two–hour 
double–length class. 
 
Lunch (1.15–2pm) 
Lunch is as elaborate as 
breakfast. Menu items include 
stews, casseroles, and pies. 
Those with a lighter appetite 
can choose from a selection of 
sandwiches and homemade 
crisps. There are also desserts 
like ice cream, apple pie, 
treacle tart, and eclairs. 
 
Afternoon (2–4pm) 
The day continued with two 
classes or a double–class 
followed by a two–hour gap 
for homework. 
 
Homework (4–6pm) 
Two–hours provided for 
individual study in the Study 
Hall next to the library, 
supervised by Madam Pince. 
Evening (5:00 PM onwards) 
Students have extravagant 
dinners in the Great Hall. 
After dinner, students can 
relax in their House Common 
Rooms to study, socialise, or 
participate in extracurricular 
activities like clubs, duelling, 
or Quidditch practice. 
A curfew is in place. The exact 
time varies by year. First–years 
should be in bed by 9pm. 
Later years have extended or 
open bedtimes, but must stay 
in their common rooms or 
bedrooms and away from 
school corridors. 
Weekends
Saturday and Sunday are 
usually free of scheduled 
classes. The library is open, 
and students have more free 
time for other activities. 
Third–years and higher 
students can visit Hogsmeade 
village (see pages 149–150).

Hogwarts: A living Castle
Hogwarts is not just the 
backdrop to your magical 
education. It is, in many ways, 
a living organism. Treating 
Hogwarts as a character in its 
own right, transforms it from a 
static map into a wilful 
presence with its own 
eccentricities, moods, and 
strange habits. 
At its most obvious, the castle 
can shift and move. Staircases 
rearrange themselves, mostly 
on schedule, but sometimes on 
a whim. A staircase that 
reliably connects the Entrance 
Hall to the fourth floor may 
decide, on a rainy Tuesday, to 
deposit travellers somewhere 
else. Rooms appear only when 
needed, or vanish arbitrarily. 
Over time, students learn the 
castle’s rhythms. A corridor 
may take longer to traverse at 
night than during the day. 
A locked door might decide to 
be unlocked when there is an 
R in the month. Centuries of 
accumulated enchantments 
have shaped Hogwarts into an 
erratic guardian of the staff and 
students who live within it. 
Portraits are the castle’s eyes 
and ears. They exchange 
secrets, whispering to each 
other in the dead of night, or 
communicating through their 
portrait in some distant place. 
A portrait’s subject can even 
move between canvases within 
the castle. Some portraits act as 
gatekeepers, demanding 
passwords or a willing 
audience before allowing 
passage. Others act as informal 
intelligence brokers, alerting 
staff to trouble or quietly 
warning students they favour. 
Not all portraits are loyal to the 
school. Many are loyal only to 
themselves, their House, or 
long–dead grudges. They 
exaggerate, dissemble, and 
misremember, freely. 
House ghosts provide another 
layer of personality. Each ghost 
embodies a particular 
interpretation of their House’s 
ideals (see pages 153–154), 
frozen at the moment of their 
tragic end. They often try to 
guide students, remembering 
past generations vividly and 
judging current students 
against those past standards. 
Importantly, ghosts can act 
where staff will not. Passing 
through sealed doors, 
eavesdropping, revealing 
ancient secrets, or warning 
students of potential risks and 
unseen dangers. 
Magical wards form the 
castle’s immune system. 
Ancient protections regulating 
who may enter and what magic 
functions where. Crucially, 
these wards have blind spots. 
They may ignore threats posed 
by students, former students, or 
creatures deemed part of the 
school’s ecosystem. These gaps 
are not flaws, so much as the 
consequence of centuries of 
overlapping and potentially 
contradictory enchantments. 
Hogwarts castle is like a 
haphazard clockwork 
mechanism filled with 
eccentrically shifting wheels 
and cogs. When characters 
learn to act in harmony with 
the castle’s capricious rhythms, 
doors open, secret passages 
reveal themselves, and the 
one hundred and forty–two 
staircases cooperate. When you 
act against the building, with 
its various trick steps and false 
doors, it can become 
obstructive and even hostile. 
Dolores Umbridge, the sadistic 
Ministry of Magic bureaucrat 
who had a short and unpleasant 
reign as Hogwarts High 
Inquisitor, was thwarted by the 
castle when it sealed the 
Headmaster's Tower to stop her 
taking over Professor 
Dumbledore’s office. 
Living within Hogwarts can be 
as much of an education in 
surviving the Wizarding world, 
as the lessons taught in its 
classrooms.

Hogwarts: Grounds and Forest
The grounds of Hogwarts 
stretch out from the castle 
across open lawns, rolling hills, 
and down toward the large 
freshwater lake. The landscape 
is practical as well as 
picturesque. Students use the 
lawns for studying, relaxing, 
and gathering between lessons. 
Paths worn into the grass show 
the most common routes 
between the castle, Hagrid’s 
hut, and the Quidditch pitch. 
The Quidditch ground stands 
some distance from the main 
building. House matches are a 
major part of school life, 
drawing large crowds and 
contributing to house pride and 
friendly (and not so friendly) 
rivalry. Outside of match days, 
the pitch is used for practice 
sessions and team trials. 
One particularly notable tree on 
the Hogwarts grounds is the 
Whomping Willow, a very 
valuable, and very violent 
species of magical plant. 
Students used to play a game, 
trying to get near enough to 
touch the trunk. Following an 
incident where a student 
nearly lost an eye, the Willow 
was declared out of bounds. 
The Great Lake forms a natural 
boundary along one side of the 
grounds. Its depths are 
inhabited by magical beings, 
including merfolk (see page 
98) and a Giant Squid (see 
page 95). 
Hagrid, Keeper of the Keys and 
Grounds of Hogwarts, lives in 
a wooden hut at the edge of the 
Forbidden Forest, a vast and 
ancient woodland that is 
strictly out of bounds to 
students except under staff 
supervision. The forest is thick 
with trees, beech, oak, pine, 
sycamore, and yew, as well as 
knotgrass, brambles, and 
thorny undergrowth underfoot. 
There are paths, clearings, and 
meandering brooks running 
through the forest, but away 
from these the way is almost 
impassable. Especially at night, 
when hazardous tree roots and 
stumps are barely visible in the 
near blackness. 
The forest is a habitat for 
numerous magical creatures, 
including a herd of centaurs 
(see page 92), who occupy its 
deeper regions and maintain 
their own social structure and 
territories, generally resisting 
interference from the school. 
A colony of Acromantulas (see 
page 90) occupy part of the 
forest, which also shelters 
unicorns (see page 100), 
bowtruckles (see page 91), 
and nifflers (see page 99). 
Thestrals (see page 101) live in 
a paddock at the forest’s edge 
and range into the trees to 
graze. Dragons were also 
temporarily kept in the forest 
for the Tri–Wizard 
Tournament. Most surprisingly, 
a feral enchanted Ford Anglia 
car also prowls the forest (see 
page 124). 
Despite its restricted status and 
obvious dangers, the forest 
is occasionally used for 
detentions or specific lessons, 
usually under staff supervision. 
Few students would willingly 
enter the menacing forest after 
dark and they are wise not to.

Other Schools of Magic
Great Britain is not the only 
country to boast of a venerable 
institution training young 
witches and wizards. Other 
schools vary in size, traditions, 
and magical specialisations, 
and reflect the cultures in 
which they developed. Only a 
handful are widely known, and 
admission is usually limited to 
students from particular 
regions. 
Beauxbatons Academy of
Magic is located somewhere in 
the Pyrenees and serves much 
of western Europe. The 
academy is famous for its 
elegant architecture, refined 
etiquette, and strong 
reputation in charms and 
transfiguration. Compared 
with Hogwarts, Beauxbatons 
places a stronger emphasis on 
formality and discipline, and 
its students are known for 
polished wandwork and 
graceful spellcasting. 
In northern Europe stands the 
ancient Durmstrang Institute. 
Its exact location is kept 
secret, but it is generally 
believed to be in a remote 
region of Scandinavia or the 
far north of Europe. 
Durmstrang has a reputation 
for a harsher climate and a 
more austere environment 
than other schools. The 
institute historically tolerated 
the study of the Dark Arts, 
though not all students pursue 
them. The school is smaller 
than Hogwarts but known for 
producing powerful and 
disciplined spellcasters. Its 
students traditionally travel by 
a magical ship that surfaces 
from beneath the water. 
Across the Atlantic, 
Ilvermorny School of
Witchcraft and Wizardry
serves the wizarding 
population of North America. 
The castle stands on Mount 
Greylock in Massachusetts. 
Ilvermorny was founded in the 
seventeenth century and 
developed traditions distinct 
from the European schools. Its 
four houses are named after 
creatures associated with 
North American folklore; 
Thunderbird, Wampus, 
Horned Serpent, and 
Pukwudgie. Ilvermorny’s 
curriculum is broadly similar 
to Hogwarts’, though it has a 
stronger tradition of wandlore. 
In Africa, the most prominent 
magical institution is Uagadou
School of Magic, located in 
the Mountains of the Moon in 
Western Uganda. Uagadou is 
described as the largest 
wizarding school in the world 
and accepts students from 
across the African continent. 
Unlike many European 
schools, Uagadou places great 
emphasis on wandless magic. 
Students are also renowned 
for their skill in astronomy, 
alchemy, and the art of 
transforming themselves into 
animals. Admission letters are 
traditionally delivered through 
dream visions rather than 
physical post. 
Two other schools are 
recognised internationally. 
Mahoutokoro School of
Magic is situated in an ornate 
palace built of white nephrite 
on the volcanic island of 
Minami Iwo Jima in Japan. It 
is known for extremely high 
academic standards and a 
world–class Quidditch team, 
Students wear enchanted 
robes that change colour to 
reflect both progress and 
behaviour. Meanwhile, 
Castelobruxo, hidden from 
Muggle eyes, as a magnificent, 
ruined golden temple. It 
specialises in Herbology and 
Magizoology, drawing on the 
extraordinary magical flora 
and fauna of the Amazon. 
Together, these institutions 
form a loose international 
network of magical education. 
Although each school has 
distinct customs, they share 
similar goals. Training young 
witches and wizards, 
preserving magical knowledge 
and traditions, and preparing 
students to participate in the 
hidden wizarding societies 
that exist alongside the non–
magical world.
