Combat
Forms of Combat 
There are two types of combat 
in the wizarding world: 
Duelling and Skirmishing. 
Duelling occurs where two 
wizards square off against 
each other in a traditional 
formal or less formal manner. 
Skirmishing covers all other 
combat. 
Rounds 
Combat is split into three–
second rounds during which 
all the participants get to carry 
out any two of the following 
actions: 
• Move 
• Cast a spell 
• Initiate a duel 
• Skirmishing Attack 
• Take cover 
• Ready or reload a weapon 
• Any other quick action or 
part of an action 
Rolling Initiative 
At the beginning of each 
round the Narrator finds out 
the players’ intentions and 
then directs the order of play 
from a logical and story telling 
perspective. Whenever actions 
are in opposition the Narrator 
may call for an initiative roll to 
see who acts first. Both parties 
roll 1D+Speed+Skirmishing
(or 1D+Speed+Duelling if 
appropriate) with the highest 
roll acting first. 
Move 
You can move a number of 
metres equal to your Speed 
plus one other action, or use 
both actions to move and run 
at twice your Speed. 
Cast a spell 
You can use an action to cast a 
spell. Normally spell casting 
rolls use Focus, but attack and 
defence spells used in combat 
are based on your Wits. 
Spells aimed at an opponent 
(Stupefy, etc.) are also subject 
to ranged combat penalties. 
The primary defence for 
wizards is the Shield Charm, 
Protego. To use it against an 
attack you will need to have 
fully mastered the spell (see 
page 40). Even if the attacker 
beats your defence, the charm 
still reduces the magical or 
physical damage (see page 56). 
Initiate a duel 
You can challenge another 
witch or wizard to single 
combat. See page 41 for 
details about Wizard Duels. 
Skirmishing attack 
An armed, unarmed, or ranged 
attack. Anybody you attack will 
have a chance to defend by 
blocking or dodging. 
Take cover 
You can duck behind cover, 
summon or conjure an object 
to block your opponent’s 
attack, cast a protective charm, 
or obscure yourself in some 
other magical way. 
Ready or reload a weapon 
You can draw a sheathed 
sword or any stowed weapon 
with a single action, but a bow 
or an unloaded crossbow will 
normally require two actions 
(one to draw and one to load). 
Any other quick action or
part of an action 
Any other activity a character 
might carry out. A simple 
action like grabbing an object 
or opening an unlocked door 
will only take a single action, 
but more involved tasks like 
picking a lock or deciphering 
an inscription might take 
several actions per attempt to 
roll successfully. 
Free Actions 
Free actions include anything 
you can do without much 
thought while focusing on 
other actions; talking, quickly 
looking around, grabbing a 
small object, etc. Most wizards 
carry their wands out of sight, 
but still ready to be swiftly 
drawn as a free action. 
Physically defending yourself 
from attack is also considered 
to be a free action: Dodging 
attacks and blocking armed 
and unarmed attacks with a 
weapon or shield. Only 
unarmed attacks can be 
blocked unarmed. Ranged 
weapons can only be blocked 
with a shield or similar.

The Health track at the 
bottom of your 
character sheet records 
injuries affecting your mind 
and body. There are six 
physical states (from Shaken 
to Lifeless) and six mental 
states (from Rattled to 
Mindless). These indicate your 
condition after combat or 
misadventure. 
When you are physically or 
mentally injured you mark off 
one or more circles starting 
from the leftmost circle and 
working across. If you 
continue to receive injuries 
you continue to fill in circles 
until you reach the sixth circle 
which indicates potential 
death or permanent catatonia. 
For each state there is a 
penalty from +1 to +7. Add 
this penalty to the difficulty of 
any tasks you roll. If you have 
both a physical and mental 
penalty these are combined. 
For example, if you are bruised 
(+2) and confused (+5), add +7
to any targets you need to roll. 
X indicates no actions 
are possible.

For most witches and wizards 
the wand will always be their 
weapon of choice, but 
occasionally other weapons 
may come to hand. Students 
at Hogwarts have even come 
to physical blows from time to 
time. For weapons damage roll 
one or more dice plus a 
modifier as shown below.

Healing 
If the right witch or wizard 
is on hand with spells like 
Episkey (minor healing) or 
Brackium Emendo (mends 
broken bones), many injuries 
can be dealt with almost 
instantly, but you still might 
find yourselves waking up 
in the Hospital Wing or 
St Mungo's Hospital. 
The injured character may 
take a long time to heal (or 
even wake up). Use the table 
below as a guide for recovery 
from physical and mental 
injuries: 
One circle (shaken or rattled) 
can be shrugged off in 
seconds, so it will only be one 
or two rounds before you 
regain your composure. Two 
circle injuries (bruised or 
dazed) last for minutes (20
rounds plus). Three or more 
circles starts to get serious. 
The Narrator shouldn’t 
underplay serious injuries, 
but also not let them get in 
the way of the story. Death 
or madness should rarely be 
certain, but should be mildly 
possible. Remember “To the 
well–organised mind, death is but 
the next great adventure.”
Temporary 
Mental States 
Some spells and experiences 
place your mind in a 
temporary mental state. For 
example, the spell Confundo 
places the victim in a confused 
state for one or more rounds, 
and the fear caused by 
witnessing certain magical 
creatures can leave you rattled, 
dazed, or worse (see page 89). 
While in a particular mental 
state the penalty for that state 
adds to all targets rolled and 
combines with any physical 
penalty. For example, in a 
Confused state add +5 to any 
target. If you are also bruised 
with a +2 penalty you face a 
total of +7 added to any targets 
you need to roll. 
If no duration is specified for 
a temporary mental state, or 
the victim is unable to shrug 
off the effect, then the spell 
wears off by one circle each 
round – confused becomes 
drained, drained becomes 
dazed, dazed becomes rattled, 
and so on – until the victim is 
entirely unaffected. The effects 
of fear caused by witnessing 
terrifying magical creatures 
also wears off in this way. 
If a spell is cast that changes 
your mental state when you 
are already in a worse one, 
then the spell will have no 
additional effect. Confundo, 
for example, will make no 
significant difference to 
somebody who is already 
raving or mindless. 
Permanent 
Mental States 
Some ordeals can leave a 
permanent mark. Misapplied 
memory charms can be highly 
debilitating, and prolonged 
use of the Cruciatus Curse 
(also known as the Torture 
Curse) can have long term 
psychological effects. 
Here are some examples from 
the Harry Potter books: 
• Ministry employee Bertha 
Jorkins was the victim of a 
powerful memory charm 
that left her hugely forgetful 
(mental state Confused). 
• Barty Crouch, Head of the 
Department of International 
Magical Cooperation, was 
left incoherent (mental state 
Raving) after fighting off the 
effects of Lord Voldermort’s 
Imperius Curse. 
• Frank and Alice 
Longbottom, the Auror 
parents of Neville 
Longbottom, were tortured 
to insanity (mental state 
Mindless) by a group of 
Death Eaters. They remain 
in St. Mungo’s Hospital to 
this day. 
Unlike physical injury, 
recovery from permanent 
emotional or mental injury is 
as difficult in the wizarding 
world as it is in the Muggle 
one. Recovery is a long 
process, if it is at all possible. 
Permanent mental injury 
should be used rarely and only 
for strong story reasons.

Pursuit 
Chasing opponents on foot, 
on beasts (magical or 
mundane), or on brooms or 
Muggle vehicles is a staple of 
dramatic storytelling. Every 
type of locomotion has a die 
roll speed. 
Running pursuers and their 
target roll 1D + Vitality +
Speed. Pursuers on foot 
cannot chase a target on a 
broomstick or other vehicle, 
only a running target. 
Broomstick speeds are listed 
on page 166, and Muggle 
vehicle speeds and flying 
magical creatures are detailed 
on page 167. 
When a pursuer is chasing a 
target, they both roll their 
speed dice and compare their 
scores using the table below.

Falling 
A character who falls more 
than two metres suffers 
damage equal to 1D per metre 
fallen. 
A house, like Harry Potter’s 
childhood home on Privet 
Drive, or most of the dwellings 
in Hogsmeade, will be about 
three metres tall per storey. 
A commercial building, or a 
large stone building like 
Hogwarts, will be more like 
four metres per storey. Most 
broomstick flying takes place 
at heights of 5 to 10 metres. 
A Quidditch goal is about 
fifteen metres off the ground, 
but many Seekers will fly 
much lower during a match. 
A fall onto soft ground, like 
deep snow, swampy earth, or 
ground softened by the 
Spongify Charm (see page 58), 
reduces damage by 2D. Impact 
with hard ground, like stone 
floors or rocks, adds +1D 
damage. The Molliare Charm 
(see page 54) reduces any 
damage by 3D. The Arresto 
Momentum spell, also known 
as the Slowing Charm (see 
page 47), reduces any damage 
to zero. Other spells that can 
be used in a plummeting 
emergency include; Descendo 
(see page 50), the Flying 
Charm (see page 52), Inflatus 
(see page 53), Levicorpus (see 
page 53), and Wingardium 
Leviosa (see page 59). The 
Summoning Charm, Accio 
(see page 47), can be used to 
bring your broomstick if it is 
close enough to save you.

Drowning 
A character who is fully 
submerged and unable to 
breathe begins to drown. 
With time to prepare, 
a character can hold their 
breath for a number of rounds 
equal to their Vitality + 10. 
Strenuous activity or panic 
reduces this by up to half at 
the Narrators’s discretion. A 
character is panicking if they 
roll 1D + Wits + Swimming
and roll less than 10. 
Unless they’re impeded in 
some way, a character in 
danger of drowning will 
attempt to swim up to the air. 
Their progress will depend on 
their depth measured in 
Fathoms. A six foot Depth. 
For example, near the shore, 
Hogwart’s Black Lake is one to 
three Fathoms deep. At the 
centre of the lake, in open 
waters, it is ten to twenty 
Fathoms deep. Each round the 
submerged character attempts 
to surface, they roll 1D +
Vitality + Swimming. If they 
roll 9+, they rise by one 
fathom. 
If a character is submerged 
and can no longer hold their 
breath, they must resist 1D 
damage. Each subsequent 
round the damage they must 
resist increases by +1D. 
Eating Gillyweed allows you to 
breath underwater for an hour 
or so (see page 69). 
The Bubble Head Charm is 
another useful survival 
option (see page 48). 
Venom and Poisons 
Where attacks have a 
poisonous effect, like a snake 
bite, they continue to cause 
damage to a character in 
subsuquent rounds until they 
become benign or are treated. 
There are three levels of 
lethality for venoms and 
poisons. Each round following 
a successful attack, the 
character must roll 1D10 +
Vitality versus the poison 
damage (see page 35). If a 
poisoned character stays 
immobile and doesn’t take any 
actions, they can add +5 to 
their roll to resist damage. 
Poison ratings are listed on 
the table, below, and on 
creature descriptions: 
I. Irritating
II. Toxic 
III. Deadly 
Irritating 
Hazardous, but extremely 
survivable. Poisonous effect 
only lasts for three rounds 
after which the poison 
becomes benign. 
The maximum injury is 
“Broken” (see page 35). 
Toxic 
Dangerous without rapid 
intervention. Normally 
treatable with a bezoar (a 
stone from the stomach of a 
goat that acts as a powerful 
antidote). The Bezoar is simply 
placed in the victim’s mouth. 
However, some toxins will 
require specialist antidotes. 
Every hour, the victim must 
save against 2D damage or 
mark off circles on their 
Body track (see page 35). 
An hourly draught of Blood 
Replenishment potion (see 
page 62) can keep the 
poisoning at bay until an 
antidote is found. 
 
Deadly 
Fatal Without Magical Aid. 
Death is rapid and virtually 
certain. If treatable, these 
always require a very specialist 
antidote. Basilisk venom, for 
example, can only be treated 
with Phoenix tears. Every 
round, unless an antidote is 
readily available, the victim 
must save against 3D damage 
or mark off circles on their 
Body track (see page 35).
