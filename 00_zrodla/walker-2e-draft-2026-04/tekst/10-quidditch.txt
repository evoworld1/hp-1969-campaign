Quidditch
Overview 
Quidditch is the most popular 
and well-known game among 
wizards and witches. 
Quidditch is played by two 
teams on broomsticks. The 
object of the game is to score 
more points than the opposing 
team. Each team has seven 
members; 
• Three Chasers who 
handle the Quaffle, a red 
leather ball, twelve inches 
in diameter. Chasers. earn 
points by tossing it 
through any one of a set 
of three goalposts on the 
opposing team’s end of 
the field. Each goal is 
worth 10 points. 
• Two Beaters armed with 
heavy wooden bats. Their 
job is to redirect the 
Bludgers, two black iron 
balls bewitched to fly 
around and try to knock 
the players off of their 
broomsticks. 
• One Keeper whose job is 
to guard their team’s 
three goalposts and stop 
the opposing team from 
scoring with the Quaffle. 
• One Seeker whose job is 
to catch the Golden 
Snitch, a walnut–sized 
winged golden sphere. 
The Snitch flies around 
the field at high speed, 
sometimes pausing and 
hovering in place. The 
game ends when one of 
the Seekers catches the 
Snitch which is worth 150
points. This almost always 
wins the match. 
Quidditch pitches are oval–
shaped, five–hundred feet long 
and one–hundred and eighty 
feet wide. At the centre of the 
pitch is a small circle, two feet 
in diameter, from where the 
balls are released at the start 
of the game. At each end of 
the pitch there are three 
hooped goal posts of different 
heights, surrounded by a 
scoring area. 
 
The game starts with the 
referee releasing all four balls 
from the central circle on the 
field. The Bludgers and Snitch 
are bewitched to fly off of 
their own accord, but the 
Quaffle is thrown into the air 
by the referee to signal the 
start of play. 
Quidditch Fandom 
Quidditch is as popular as 
football is in the Muggle world 
and most witches and wizards 
support a team. One–
hundred–thousand fans 
attended the Quidditch World 
Cup in 1994. There are 
fourteen teams in the British 
and Irish League (see right), 
over forty International teams, 
and twenty–seven Regional 
teams around the world. The 
International Confederation of 
Wizards' Quidditch 
Committee is the sport’s 
international regulatory body. 
Quidditch is governed in 
Britain by the Department of 
Magical Games and Sports, 
where the British and Irish 
Quidditch League 
Headquarters are based. 
 
British fans keep up to date 
with the latest Quidditch news 
in the Daily Prophet and Seeker 
Weekly magazine. Dedicated 
fans can signal their support 
through a wide range of 
merchandis, including hats, 
scarves, flags, jerseys, 
enchanted badges, and moving 
posters and figurines. 
 
The Inter–House Quidditch 
Cup at Hogwarts School is a 
mainstay of rivalry between 
the four houses who play each 
other throughout the school 
year. Three games for each 
team, and six games of 
Quidditch for the school to 
enjoy overall. Each match is 
eagerly anticipated and mostly 
attended by the entire school, 
including excited professors.

Preparation 
At the beginning of the match 
the narrator decides the 
current weather, or rolls it 
randomly (see below). Each 
weather type has a rating 
ranging from I (bright and 
calm) to VIII (dark and 
turbulent). Each level has 
a modifier that affects the 
Seekers’ chance to catch the 
Golden Snitch. Weather 
conditions can improve or 
worsen during the course of 
the game. 
At the start of the match, each 
of the three Chasers on both 
teams starts with two Quaffle
Tokens. You can use pennies 
(Knuts), Bertie Bott's Every 
Flavour Beans, paper chits, or 
the cut–out tokens on page 
@@. These tokens represent 
control of the Quaffle. At the 
beginning of the game, neither 
side has control, so both teams 
are matched. Successful plays 
by a team’s Seekers grab 
Quaffle tokens from the other 
side. When a team has nine 
tokens, they are in a position 
to try and score. 
At the start of play, team 
captains toss to see who plays 
North, Forbidden Forest end, 
and who plays South, 
Hogwarts Castle end (see 
below). The winner of the toss 
plays North. This doesn’t 
directly effect play, but allows 
the rules to generically refer to 
the teams as North or South.

The Round 
At the start of each round the 
narrator rolls 1D10 for Match 
Events (see below). The result 
will decide which element of 
the match is the focus of that 
round; the Chasers, The 
Beaters, or the Seekers. There 
is also a Keeper Focus that 
kicks in if one team gains 
control of the Quaffle. Think 
of this as the attention of the 
crowd watching the match. 
 
All eyes are focussed 
on the Chasers as they 
zig–zag across the pitch. 
Suddenly the Seekers sight 
the Golden Snitch and 
zoom off in pursuit with all 
eyes following their progress. 
The roll can also indicate a 
change in the weather, 
improving or worsening 
conditions. A weather result 
also triggers the Chaser Focus.
Match Events (Roll 1D10)
1 – 2 Normal play. Chaser Focus. Page 81.
3 – 4 Bludger attack. Beater Focus, page 83 (roll for Bludger Target below).
5 – 7 Golden Snitch sighted. Seeker Focus. Page 83.
8 Weather improves (reduce level by one point). Chaser Focus. Page 81.
9 Weather deteriorates (increase level by one point). Chaser Focus. Page 81.
10 Weather worsens (increase level by two points). Chaser Focus. Page 81.

Chaser Focus 
1. The team Captains roll for 
initiative: 1D+Wits+ 
Strategy (re–roll ties). 
The team with the highest 
roll become the attackers 
with the other side acting 
as the defenders. 
2. Both sides secretly select 
an attack or defence 
manoeuvre depending on 
their current status 
(attacker or defender). 
The team captain decides 
which Chaser is 
attempting the manoeuvre. 
Both sides then reveal 
their manoeuvre 
simultaneously. 
3. Result depends on who 
rolls higher (re–roll ties) 
and the manoeuvre both 
sides have chosen (see 
right). Attacker/defender 
may win Quaffle Tokens 
from the opposing team, 
gain a bonus to initiative 
next round, gain penalty 
points, pass Quaffle Tokens 
between Chasers, or 
potentially lose control of 
their broom. 
4. When a team has nine or 
more Quaffle Tokens, or 
five or more tokens are 
held by a single Chaser, 
that team can attempt to 
score a goal (see page 84). 
5. Repeat the round (starting. 
from step 2, above) with 
attacker becoming 
defender and vice–versa. 
When both sides have had 
a turn as attacker, start a 
New Round (page 80).
There are four attacking manoeuvres (Tackle, Shadow, Feint, 
or Foul) and four defensive manoeuvres (Evade, Pass, Feint, or 
Reverse). 
The manoeuvre can be secretly written on a piece of paper or 
you can use the print and cut–out chits on page 171.

Beater Focus 
1. Narrator rolls 1D10 to 
identify the target (see 
below). 
2. Beater rolls 1D+Vitality+
Balance. Rolling 10+ 
deflects the Bludger from 
its target. Rolling 15+ 
directs it towards a target 
of the Beater’s choice. 
3. The target of Bludger rolls 
1D + Wits + 
Broomstick Riding. 
If the Bludger has been 
diverted by a Beater, the 
target must roll higher 
than the Beater to Dodge. 
If the Bludger has not 
been redirected by a 
Beater, the target must roll 
10+ to dodge. 
4. Both beaters can 
simultaneously strike the 
Bludger (the Dopplebeater 
Defence). Both Beaters roll 
and their target must roll 
higher than the lower of 
the two Beaters’ rolls. If 
the Dopplebeater Defence 
is successful, the Bludger 
causes +1 Injury Point (two 
points instead of three). 
See page @@ to find out 
about Injury Points. 
5. After dealing with the 
result of any injuries, start 
a New Round (page 80). 
Seeker Focus 
Seekers have a pursuit track 
that they mark off as they get 
closer to catching the Golden 
Snitch. When a seeker ticks 
off the seventh circle, they 
have caught the snitch. 
1. Both Seekers roll 1D +
Wits + Observation minus 
the visibility penalty, 
depending on the weather 
(see page 79). A Seeker 
who rolls 15+ has spotted 
the fast moving Snitch. 
2. If one Seeker fails to spot 
the Snitch, their round 
ends here. If both Seekers 
fail to sight it, start a 
New Round (page 80). 
3. If both Seekers spot the 
Snitch, they roll 1D +
Speed + Broomstick
Flying. If one Seeker has a 
faster broom, they can add 
a bonus equal to the 
difference between the two 
speed ratings (see below). 
The Seeker who rolls 
higher (re–roll ties) than 
the other Seeker, crosses 
off a circle on their Pursuit 
track. 
4. If only one of the Seekers
spots the Snitch, they must 
roll higher than the 
Snitch’s speed (1D+10) to 
cross off a circle. 
5. When a Seeker marks off 
the seventh circle on their 
pursuit track, they have 
caught the Golden Snitch. 
They scores 150 points for 
their team, and the Match
Ends* 
6. If neither Seeker has 
completed their Pursuit 
Track, start a New Round
(page 80). 
* When the match ends, the 
team with the most points 
wins. In the great majority of 
Quidditch matches, the 150
points scored by catching 
the Snitch wins the game, 
but not always.

Keeper Focus 
The Keeper comes into play 
when a team gains nine 
Quaffle tokens, or one Chaser 
on the team holds five or more 
tokens (see page 81). 
1. The Chaser with most 
Quaffle Tokens rolls to 
score (in the event of a tie, 
the team Captain selects 
the Chaser to make the 
try). 
2. The Chaser rolls 1D +
Speed + Balance. 
The Keeper rolls 1D +
Wits + Observation. 
3. If the Keeper rolls higher, 
they avert the goal. 
If the Chaser rolls higher, 
they score the goal and 
their team gains 10 points. 
If both roll equally, the 
Quaffle bounces off the 
goalpost. 
4. The Keeper takes one 
Quaffle Token from a 
Chaser on the opposing 
team and hands it (throws 
it out) to a Chaser on their 
own team. 
5. If the opposing team still 
have enough Quaffle 
Tokens to attempt to score, 
then repeat the above 
steps. Otherwise, start a 
New Round (page 80). 
Mishaps 
All players can be injured 
during the game. Either 
because of Bludger attacks or 
broom misadventures. In 
addition, chasers can gain 
Penalty Points when they try 
to foul their opponents (see 
page 81). 
Injuries 
For speed of play, the 
Quidditch rules use a 
simplified injury system. 
A set numbers of injury points 
apply to Bludger collisions 
and broom accidents, 
depending on the seriousness 
of the misadventure. A near 
scrape or a full crash: 
The first two circles have a 
penalty, +1,+2, or +3, which 
corresponds with the injury 
track on the player’s 
character sheet. The 
penalty is added to any 
target they need to roll 
during the match. 
If a player fills in all three 
circles on their injury track, 
they are out of the game for 
three rounds while being 
magically healed. 
Rub out an injury point at the 
beginning of each round that 
they are incapacitated. 
Once their injury track is clear, 
they can return to the match 
at the start of the following 
round. If a Keeper or a Seeker 
is put out of action, a Beater or 
Chaser is nominated by the 
Captain to take their place 
until they return to the match. 
If a player finishes the game 
with one or more points of 
injury, they transfer to the 
circles on the Body track on 
the Health section of their 
character sheet (see page 35). 
Penalties 
Chasers may earn a Penalty 
Point when they attempt to 
foul an opponent (see page 
81). When a Chaser has three 
Penalty Points, they are sent 
off for one round, but any 
further Penalty Points will 
result in that Chaser’s ejection 
for the remainder the match. 
For every two Penalty Points 
a team earns between them, 
their opponent is rewarded a 
free shot, triggering the 
Keeper Focus for the other 
team (see page 84).In the event 
of a free shot, the Keeper does 
not take a Quaffle Token to 
pass to their team following 
the goal attempt (see page 84).
