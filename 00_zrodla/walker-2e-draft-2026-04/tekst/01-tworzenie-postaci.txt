Character Lineage
1. Choose a Name 
Names in the wizarding world 
tend to mix the traditional and 
mundane with the eccentric 
and mythological. You may 
want to decide your lineage 
(see right) before choosing a 
name so that you can select 
one that fits your background. 
Muggle–borns generally have 
more ordinary names 
(Hermione Granger, Myrtle 
Warren, Colin Creevey). Many 
Half–bloods also fairly 
conventional (Harry Potter, 
Maisie Cattermole, Seamus 
Finnigan, Dean Thomas), but 
many are more elaborate 
(Albus Dumbledore, Gilderoy 
Lockhart, Quirinus Quirrell, 
Nymphadora Tonks). 
Pure–bloods draw on a 
specific pool of ancestral 
names and many wouldn’t be 
caught dead with a Muggle 
name (Draco Malfoy, Bellatrix 
Lestrange, Sirius Black, 
Marvolo Gaunt), but with the 
dilution of the Pure–bloods, 
many are surprisingly down to 
earth (Ron Weasley, Neville 
Longbottom, Lavender 
Brown). 
We have fewer examples of 
Part–human characters, but 
they are equally flamboyant; 
(Rubeus Hagrid, Filius 
Flitwick, Fleur Delacour, 
and Lorcan d'Eath). 
If you need inspiration, 
there are tables to roll 
wizarding name on 
pages 163–164. 
2. Decide on your 
 Lineage 
Not all witches and wizards are 
created equal. Pure–blood 
wizards come from old 
Wizarding families. Some of 
which guard their privilege 
fiercely, but the number of 
pure–bloods is vanishingly 
small. Most wizards are half–
bloods, having parents or 
grandparents split between 
Wizard and Muggle ancestry. 
Occasionally a witch or wizard 
is born to two Muggle parents 
who have a Wizarding 
ancestor somewhere in the 
family tree. Muggle–born 
witches and wizards are as 
magically talented as any other 
wizard (sometimes more so), 
but are looked down upon by 
haughty Pure–bloods. 
You can choose your lineage 
or roll on the table below. 
Part–human characters face 
daily prejudice and distrust in 
the Wizarding World, 
particularly from 
Pureblood wizard families. 
For every fair–minded 
individual like Albus 
Dumbledore, there 
are a hundred 
wizards who would 
happily see you 
hounded out of 
wizard society. 
Muggle–born 
Sometimes referred to by the 
insulting and offensive 
pejorative Mudblood, a 
Muggle–born is a witch or 
wizard born to two non–
magical parents. Their magical 
abilities are unimpeded by 
their Muggle parentage. In 
fact, many Muggle–borns like 
Hermione Granger are among 
the most talented witches and 
wizards of their age. 
Muggle–born wizards may 
begin the game with the 
Muggle Studies skill (see page 
22) depending on their age: 
Half–blood 
Wizards who have either one 
Muggle or Muggle–born 
parent, two Half–blood 
parents, or Muggle or Muggle–
born grandparents. Some of 
the more zealous Pure–bloods 
treat Half–bloods as inferiors, 
considering themselves to be 
inherently more powerful, but 
some of the most powerful 
wizards and witches, like 
Dumbledore, are half-blood. 
Because of their wizarding 
lineage and often eventful 
childhoods, Half–bloods begin 
the game with 3 Story Points 
(see page 4).

to have no Muggles, Muggle￾borns, or Half-bloods in their 
family tree; however, most 
wizards and witches consider 
an individual to be a pure￾blood if neither his or her 
parents nor grandparents were 
Muggles or Muggle-borns. 
The most illustrious Pure–
blood families are the Sacred 
Twenty-Eight. Wizarding 
dynasties listed in the Pure–
Blood Directory published in 
the 1930s. 
Roll for your family name. 
Each family has specific skills 
that your character begins the 
game with (see page 13), 
including differing levels of 
Prestige connected to their 
exalted name. 
Some families are marked 
Inert because their line is 
extinct or reduced to a single 
member. 
You may find yourself 
connected to prominent 
individuals in the wizarding 
world, but you’re only likely to 
be a first or second cousin, so 
don’t expect any special 
treatment.

Part–humans
Part–humans 
Characters with one human 
and one non–human parent 
inherit some of their non–
human parent’s traits. 
Half–Elf 
A House–Elf, or more 
correctly an Elf, is a magical 
being devoted to serving their 
master or masters. House-elves 
tend to be bonded to old 
wizarding families and can 
only be freed when their 
master present them with 
clothes. Their offspring, Part– 
elves, are entirely free, but do 
frequently fixate on a family or 
group of friends. Part–Elf’s do 
not need wands and suffer no 
penalty for windless magic. In 
fact they gain a +3 bonus 
when using a wand. Half–Elf’s 
are also immune to spells that 
inhibit Apparition; the Anti–
Apparition Charm and the 
Anti–Disapparition Jinx 
(see page 47) and to 
spells that block or 
detect entry; the Anti–
intruder Jinx (see page 
47), the Caterwauling 
Charm (see page 48) 
and the Intruder 
Charm (see page 53). 
Half–Giant 
beings with half giant heritage 
and half human. Rubeus 
Hagrid had a giant mother and 
a wizard father. Half–Giants 
share the blood heritage of 
their ancestors which grants 
them a degree of spell 
resistance. When resisting 
aimed spells (see page 41), 
a Half–Giant has one or more 
dice of magical armour (see 
right). 
From birth, a Half–Giant has 
1D of magical protection. 
At the age of twelve they gain 
an extra dice and an additional 
dice every two years until they 
reach the age of eighteen (see 
right). 
Half–Giants also have greater 
strength and endurance than 
the equivalent human, gaining 
a bonus point of Vitality at 
various ages as shown on the 
chart below, along with their 
magical resistance. 
The bonuses are cumulative, 
so if you are creating a 16
year–old Half–Giant witch 
or wizard, they would have 
4D magical armour and 
seven bonus Vitality points. 
Naturally, Half–Giants are 
extremely tall and broad. 
At birth they are no different 
in size to a human baby, but 
will ultimately grow to 
between 8 to 13 feet tall. 
Hagrid was taller than his 
wizard father at the age of six, 
and was already around 
eleven feet tall at the start of 
his teens. Growing to eleven 
and a half feet in adulthood.

Part–humans
Part–Goblin 
Of Goblin ancestry, Part–
Goblins are much shorter 
than humans, but frequently 
quicker witted. 
When you create a part–
Goblin character add +2
points to their Wits attribute 
(see page 12) and +3 to their 
Crafting skill (see page 21). 
Part–Goblins are allowed to 
carry wands and perform 
wand magic, and are not 
covered by the Wand Ban that 
prohibits full Goblins from 
using wands. Professor Filius 
Flitwick, Charms Master at 
Hogwarts, is part–Goblin and 
a formidable wizard and 
duelling champion, so Goblin 
heritage is no bar to wizarding 
prowess. 
Goblins possess innate 
metalworking magic. When a 
Part–Goblin is crafting a 
metal, or largely metal, object 
for enchantment (see page 71), 
they gain a +5 bonus to their 
Crafting Score (see page 73). 
Part–Veela 
Veela are magical beings 
native to Bulgaria who are 
young and beautiful, with 
white-gold hair and moon￾bright skin. Their appearance 
and their movements are 
hypnotically seductive, but 
they can transform into fierce 
bird–like creatures like 
Harpies when angered. 
Veela hair can be used as a 
wand core (see page 29) and 
pairs well with Cherry, 
Redwood, and Rosewood. 
Despite the lack of their 
antecedent’s siren like 
abilities, part–Veela begin 
with 5 points in their 
Persuasion skill (see 
page 23), 2 points in the 
Authority skill (see page 21) 
and, because of their charm, 
2 points in the Magical 
Creatures skill (see page 22). 
Part–Vampire 
Muggle vampires may bestially 
rip open the necks of their 
prey, but wizarding vampires 
are far more civilised, using a 
variation of the Tergeo spell to 
siphon the blood from their 
victims. Modern vampires 
more often use Transfiguration 
to turn water, or wine, or some 
other potable liquid into a 
facsimile of blood. Part–
Vampires don’t have a need to 
drink blood, but may still 
imbibe at family gatherings. 
Part–Vampires are pale and 
find very bright sunlight 
uncomfortable. They also 
retain some of the physical 
advantages of their ancestors. 
When you create a part–
Vampire character add +2
points to their Speed attribute 
(see page 12). They also begin 
with +3 points in their Stealth 
skill (see page 23).

Rare Traits
3. Rare Traits 
Some abilities are are highly 
uncommon among wizard 
kind. Roll to see if you are 
lucky (or unlucky) enough to 
possess one of these traits. 
Wand Versatility
You can use other wizard’s 
wands without gaining the 
wand’s allegiance. The penalty 
for using another wizards 
wand (see page 41) is reduced 
by five points. 
Seer
You have the ability to see into 
the future with your Inner 
Eye. Seeing into the future is 
incredibly difficult because of 
the complexity of every single 
action and their consequences. 
Visions are frequently unclear 
and wildly open to 
misinterpretation, and not all 
seers have control over their 
power. Whenever the Narrator 
awards you with a vision, roll 
1D10+Focus+Divination. 
If you roll 16+, you retain 
awareness of the outside world 
and can remember your 
predictions. If you fail the roll, 
although the people around 
you will hear your prophecy, 
once you leave the trance state 
you will have no idea that 
anything out of the ordinary 
happened. 
 
Metamorphmagus
You have a rare magical ability 
to change you physical 
appearance at will, without the 
need for Polyjuice Potion, 
Transfiguration spell, or other 
external means. You can 
change your appearance to 
exactly resemble anybody you 
have a chance to observe, 
including ghosts, or alter it to 
disguise your appearance. 
You still need to roll 
1D10+Wits+Disguise to 
mimic their personality and 
behaviour. You can substitute 
Chicanery or Art (Acting) for 
Disguise, if those skills are 
higher. A roll of 5+ will fool 
somebody who barely knows 
the person you are mimicking, 
10+ for somebody who knows 
them well, and 15+ for 
somebody who knows them 
intimately (a family member, 
for example). These abilities 
are affected by your emotional 
state, and you cannot use this 
ability if your mental state is 
“Drained” or worse (see page 
35). 
 
Parseltongue
You can speak Parseltongue, 
the hissing language of 
serpents (as well as other 
magical serpentine creatures, 
like the Runespoor and 
Basilisk). A wizard or witch 
who speaks Parseltongue is 
known as a Parselmouth. It is 
normally a hereditary ability 
and nearly all known 
Parselmouths are descended 
from Salazar Slytherin. The 
ability has an association with 
Dark wizards which generally 
causes fear and distrust of 
those with the ability. 
 
Lycanthropy
You were bitten by a werewolf, 
but your wounds were treated 
with a mixture of powdered 
silver and dittany. This saved 
your life, but cursed you to 
transform into a werewolf 
during the complete rising of 
the full moon. This Lunar 
event happens once during 
each month and transforms 
you into an uncontrollable, 
fearsome, and deadly werewolf 
(see page 64). You can use the 
Wolfsbane Potion (see page 
101) to retain your human 
mind and avoid changing into 
a mindless beast. 
Werewolves suffer from the 
disdain experienced by half–
breeds. It is not uncommon 
for people known to be 
werewolves to be shunned by 
society and discriminated 
against within the wizarding 
world. For example, it is very 
difficult for a werewolf to get 
a job in the wizarding 
community.

Character Foundations
4. Choose a 
 Profession 
If you’re playing students at 
Hogwarts or one of the other 
Wizarding schools, you can 
skip this step. The Harry 
Potter RPG allows you to play 
witches and wizards at any 
stage in their school career 
and beyond. If you’re playing a 
working wizard you can 
choose one of the professions 
on pages 19–20. 
5. School, Year 
 and House 
Most characters will be 
current students or ex–
students of Hogwarts School 
of Witchcraft and Wizardry, 
but you could easily explore 
new territory with a campaign 
set in a different school, or 
mix things up with an 
exchange student from a 
different institution joining 
students at Hogwarts. There 
are two other large Schools of 
magic in Europe; Beauxbaton 
Academy in France, and The 
Durmstrang Institute in 
Eastern Europe. 
You can play a character 
from a querulous first–year or
start off later in their
academic life (see page 17).
You can even jump straight
into playing a fully–qualified
adult witch or wizard (see
page 20). Where you start will
depend on the story that the
Narrator wants to tell, so
check with them first. 
Fill in your school (in most 
cases, Hogwarts), your year 
and House. If your Character 
has left Hogwarts just put the 
year they left. Alternatively, not 
every member of the 
wizarding world will have 
attended a school of magic in 
which case just leave this part 
of the character sheet blank. 
New students are placed into 
their Houses (see right) by the 
Narrator in the guise of the 
Sorting Hat (see page 152). 
Players will generally be in the 
same House because they will 
share the same common room 
and attend the same classes. 
Houses share lessons but are 
insular and often harbour 
rivalries that can make 
cooperative play very tricky. 
Hogwarts has four School 
Houses (see page 153): 
Gryffindor 
Founded by Godric 
Gryffindor. Their values are 
bravery, daring, and chivalry. 
Its emblem is the Lion. 
Hufflepuff 
Founded by Helga Hufflepuff, 
Their values are hard work, 
patience, loyalty, and fair play. 
Its emblem is the Badger. 
Ravenclaw 
Founded by Rowena 
Ravenclaw. Their values are 
intelligence, knowledge, and 
wit. Its emblem is the Eagle. 
Slytherin 
Founded by Salazar Slytherin. 
Their values are ambition, 
cunning and resourcefulness. 
Its emblem is the Snake.

Define Your Attributes
What are 
attributes? 
Your attributes are qualities 
that define your character’s 
ability in five key areas. 
Vitality 
Your strength and physical 
endurance. Your life force. 
Speed 
Your reflexes and 
coordination. Your ability to 
react and move quickly. 
Wits 
Your quickness of thought and 
perception. Your analytical 
qualities. 
Knowledge 
Your wisdom and accumulated 
knowledge. Your store of 
experience. 
Focus 
Your discipline and mental 
strength. Your ability to 
control magical forces. 
Each attribute is expressed as 
a number. The higher the 
number, the better you will 
perform in that area. The 
normal range for a wizard is 
between one and ten. 
Some exceptional individuals, 
like Albus Dumbledore or 
Lord Voldemort do have 
attributes higher than ten. It is 
not impossible that you will 
attain that kind of mythic 
ability, but it would require a 
very long lived character and 
much sacrifice. 
Creating a New 
Character 
To create a new character 
divide 11 points between the 
five attributes. You must allot 
at least one point to each. 
Write the score in the 
adjacent box on your character 
sheet. For example: 
“One minute to go and he’d be 
eleven. Thirty seconds… 
twenty… ten… nine — maybe 
he’d wake Dudley up, just to 
annoy him — three… two… 
one… BOOM.” 
These 11 points defines your 
character at age eleven, 
entering your first year of 
study. At each subsequent 
birthday you can add an extra 
point to one of your attributes 
until the age of 18. 
If you create an older student 
(see pages 17–18) you will 
have extra points to add to 
your Attributes. In the same 
way, if you want to create a 
younger character you just 
reduce the number of 
Attribute points. For example, 
to create a seven year–old 
fledgling wizard you just 
divide seven points between 
the five Attributes. 
Wands 
A wand is the one 
indispensable tool of any 
witch or wizard and it is vital 
that you have a wand that suits 
your character. 
Talk through the outline of 
your character with the 
Narrator to identify the ideal 
material for your wand. 
Different woods have different 
bonuses. If you want to play an 
innately good wizard, a wand 
with a proclivity for the dark 
arts may not be the best 
choice. On the other hand, not 
everybody starts with their 
ideal wand. Many young 
wizards have been held back 
by a hand–me–down wand. 
At the Narrator’s discretion 
you can choose your ideal 
wand, roll your wand using 
the information on pages 24 to 
30, or a combination of the 
two approaches.

All Hogwarts students are 
allowed a pet. First year 
students are only permitted 
a Cat, Owl, or Toad. Older 
students may choose more 
exotic animals. Choose one 
or use the table below.

Skills 
The skills section of your 
character sheet lists commonly 
encountered skills in the 
wizarding world (see pages 21–
23 for skill descriptions) and 
an optional blank line for a 
less common skill that makes 
your character unique. Skills 
begin at zero points. You can 
add points to skills during 
character creation (see right) 
or during play using Story 
Points (see page 31). When a 
skill reaches 5 points or above, 
you can choose a specialism. 
For example: 
 Magical Creatures (Dragons) 
 Music (Bagpipes) 
 Riding (Horse) 
 Strategy (Wizard Chess) 
 
You gain a +1 bonus to any 
roll involving your specialism. 
For example, if you specialised 
in playing the bagpipes, you 
add +1 to your Music roll 
when playing the pipes.

If you’re entering your first 
year at Hogwarts then you 
won’t have any additional

have professional skills 
dependant on their career.

Example Character creation
Introducing, Verity 
Hopkins, First Year 
Half–blood witch. 
As a Half–blood, she doesn’t 
begin with any existing skills, 
like a Muggle–born or Pure–
blood witch, but she does 
begin with three Story Points 
(see page 4) which she marks 
at the bottom–right of her 
character sheet. 
Verity is sorted into the 
knowledge rich Ravenclaw 
House (see page 154). 
She divides eleven points 
between her attributes (see 
page 12) and puts the majority 
of her points into Wits and 
Knowledge to fit in with her 
fellow Ravenclaws. She rolls 
for her pet (see page 12) and is 
pleased to have an Owl. She 
names him Hermes. 
Verity rolls for her wand (see 
page 24–30) and receives a 
springy Chestnut wand with a 
Unicorn Hair core. Chestnut 
and Unicorn Hair are a good 
combination, so she she gets a 
slightly better bonus when 
using protective magic. 
She has twelve points to 
divide between childhood 
skills (see page 13), and 
chooses Art, Broomstick 
Flying, Observation, and 
Strategy (Quidditch). 
She puts most of her points 
into Broomstick Flying, 
hoping to be selected for the 
Ravenclaw Quidditch team. 
She also has three points to 
spend on any other skills or 
First Year magic. Most 
students don’t start with any 
magical knowledge, but she 
likes the idea of arriving with a 
spell in hand. She looks down 
the list of First Year spells (see 
page 18) and thinks that 
Flipendo, the Knockback Jinx, 
sounds like a useful spell to 
have, so she uses two of her 
points to mark off two boxes. 
She now knows the Flipendo 
spell and can use it freely. 
The last of her three points 
she uses to give her one point 
in the Archivist skill.

First Year and Beyond
Each year on 1st 
September, new and 
returning students take 
the Hogwarts Express from 
platform 9¾ at King’s Cross 
Station. You can begin your 
journey as a fledgling eleven 
year old student and play 
through your education year–
by–year, or jump straight into 
an older year. 
First Year 
You are taken by boat across 
the Black Lake to Hogwarts. 
On arrival you are sorted into 
the House that will become 
your family through your

Second Year 
Second year and higher, you 
travel from Hogsmeade Village 
to the school in a carriage

continue the Core Subjects. 
Broomstick flying lessons are

Third & Fourth Year 
As well as continuing with the 
Core Subjects, you study at

Hogsmeade on selected 
weekends. During the fourth

preparations begins for 
O.W.L. (Ordinary Wizarding

Fifth Year 
A demanding year as you work 
towards O.W.L. exams at the 
end of the year. Homework 
increases and many students 
are forced to drop an Elective. 
Your O.W.L. results will

Exhausting Wizarding Test

counselling also begins to 
help you select appropriate 
N.E.W.T.S. 
Sixth Year 
Students have more free time, 
but this is all used up with 
increased homework and 
studying for N.E.W.T. Exams. 
Students only take classes 
where O.W.L. results qualify 
them to advance to N.E.W.T. 
examinations. Specialised 
classes like Alchemy become 
available (subject to demand) 
and students can start taking 
Apparition lessons for a fee of 
twelve Galleons. 
Seventh Year 
The final year of formal 
magical education. One male 
and one female student are 
selected from seventh year to 
be Head Boy and Girl. 
Students continue their 
classes from sixth year as they 
approach their N.E.W.T. 
exams. 
 
At the end of the seventh
year, final year students
travel back across the Black
Lake to their graduation
ceremony.

made it to their final year. 
Then go on to page 19 to add 
some professional skills.

Magical Teaching 
Students at Hogwarts will 
typically spend four to six 
hours each week learning the 
Core Subjects. The main bulk 
of their time split between the 
four practical magic sessions; 
Charms, Defence Against the 
Dark Arts, Transfiguration, and 
Potions. 
The distribution of time 
between the core subjects will 
change as they advance 
through the school. In the first 
year learning is weighted 
towards Charms classes, but as 
their study becomes more 
advanced an increasing share 
of the timetable will be spent 
on the challenging mastery of 
Transfiguration and the life–
saving Defence Against the 
Dark Arts. The rigorous study 
of Potions will be maintained 
throughout their time at the 
school.

and Ministry Officers will 
normally attend all 7 years. 
Finish your character by 
dividing 10 points between the 
skills on the opposite page 
depending on your chosen 
profession.

Skill Descriptions
Alchemy 
An ancient science based 
around the properties and 
transmutation of the four 
basic elements. Alchemy is an 
elective subject taught to sixth 
and seventh-years only. 
Ancient Runes 
The study of ancient texts, 
although runes are still widely 
used by the modern magical 
community. An elective 
available from the third year. 
Ancient Studies 
The ancient origins of magic 
across the world. Including 
Ancient Egyptian and Druidic 
magic. Useful for Curse 
Breakers and Explorers. 
An elective during the sixth 
and seventh years of study. 
Archivist 
You know your way around a 
library and you’re well versed 
in locating and sifting through 
old documents, ancient tomes, 
newspaper clippings, and 
other ephemera for that vital 
snippet of information. 
Arithmancy 
The study of the complex 
mathematical properties of 
numbers. A more scientific 
method of predicting the 
future than other forms of 
divination. An elective subject 
from the third year. 
Art 
A catch all for the creative 
arts. Ranging from the 
performing arts like acting and 
dance, to visual arts like 
painting, sculpture, and wizard 
photography. An elective from 
the third year. 
Astronomy 
The study of the stars and 
movement of planets. A core 
subject during the first five 
years of study. 
Authority 
You have a position of power, 
undeniable expertise, or just 
an air of confidence. You can 
galvanise and dominate those 
around you. Useful for 
inspiring others and quelling 
cacophonous classrooms. 
Balance 
Your physical coordination 
and equilibrium. Useful for 
climbing, jumping, throwing 
and catching, tightrope 
walking, and many other 
physical feats. 
Broomstick Flying 
First year flying lessons cover 
broom summoning and 
mounting, liftoff and landing, 
and basic flying. Second and 
third year lessons are optional, 
The second year covers fast 
laps and weaving. Third year 
includes rolling, diving, and 
loop–the–loop. 
Charms 
Charms are a set subject for 
the first five years of your 
magical training. Charms 
account for the majority of 
spells and are more adaptable 
than Transfiguration. Charms 
are less rigorous and exacting 
than Transfiguration, but are 
equally powerful. 
Chicanery 
Deception, subterfuge, sharp 
practice, and fast talk. Any 
attempt to bamboozle for fun 
or profit is covered by this 
skill, from minor mischief to 
grand larceny. You also have a 
good eye for spotting double–
dealing and duplicity. 
Concealment 
Hiding yourself and allies, 
palming objects, erasing signs 
of passage, and obscuring 
anything that might give you 
away. Concealment and 
Disguise is a course taught 
during Auror training. 
Crafting 
Covers a range of constructive 
crafts; woodworking and 
metalworking, carving, lens 
grinding, book binding, and so 
on. Useful in creating wands 
and other magical artefacts. 
Can also be used to modify 
objects, including Muggle 
items, but this may fall foul of 
the Misuse of Muggle Artefacts 
Office and land you in hot 
water with the Ministry of 
Magic.

Skill Descriptions
Dark Arts 
Also known as Dark Magic, 
the Dark Arts revolve around 
cruel and malicious magics 
that seek to harm and control. 
Dark Magic is inherently 
corrupting. The Dark Arts are 
part of the permanent 
curriculum at Durmstrang, but 
only taught at Hogwarts when 
Voldemort controlled the 
Ministry of Magic between 
1997 and 1998. 
Defence Against 
the Dark Arts 
(D.A.D.A.) 
Techniques taught to defend 
against the Dark Arts (curses, 
hexes, and jinxes), and dark 
creatures. Training includes 
Counter–Spells, and minor 
curses and jinxes for duelling. 
Disguise 
Impersonation and stagecraft 
to hide your identity or, with 
sufficient skill, appear to be 
someone else. Concealment 
and Disguise is a course 
taught during Auror training. 
Divination 
Rituals and tools to divine the 
future, including; crystal ball 
scrying, astrology and 
horoscopes, and tea leaf 
reading. Although a useful 
guide in decision making, 
divination is notoriously 
unreliable. Divination is an 
elective from the third year. 
Duelling 
Skill in the formal practise of 
Wizard Duelling both as a 
sport and in battle. Wizard 
Duelling is taught at an extra–
curricular Duelling Club, 
although groups of students 
are also free to organise their 
own ad–hoc duelling practice. 
Herbology 
The study of magical and 
mundane plants and fungi. 
The care of these plants and 
their dangers and uses. 
A core area of study. 
History of Magic 
The history of witches and 
wizards and the magical world. 
The foundations of wizarding 
society, via witch hunts, the 
Giant Wars, and Goblin 
Rebellions. A core subject. 
Law 
Knowledge of the laws and 
elaborate legal systems of the 
wizarding world that govern 
magical behaviour, preserve 
secrecy, and avoid anarchy in 
the wizarding world. 
Magical Creatures 
Practical knowledge about the 
behaviour and habitats of 
magical creatures and how to 
maintain and care for them, 
from Flobberworm to Yetis. 
Care of Magical Creatures is 
an elective course from the 
third year (see page 85). 
Magical Theory 
The theoretical foundations of 
magic, including the basics of 
how spells work. At advanced 
levels, the most talented 
wizards may use it for creating 
new spells or modifying 
existing ones. Magical Theory 
is mandatory in the first year 
at Hogwarts, but elective 
thereafter. 
Muggle Studies 
The history and daily lives of 
Muggles. Particularly their 
amazing ability to live without 
magic. Vital for blending in 
with Muggles, but taught as 
an optional subject from the 
third year. 
Music 
The study of musical 
performance and composition 
with one or more instruments. 
Music is an elective subject at 
Hogwarts from the third year 
onwards. Separate classes are 
available for wizarding and 
Muggle music. 
Observation 
Awareness and perceptiveness. 
The higher your skill level, the 
more alert and sharp eyed you 
are. Useful for spotting small 
clues, overhearing 
conversations, and avoiding 
ambushes. On a good day 
nothing escapes your notice. 
A vital skill for Aurors, 
investigators, hunters, and 
journalists.

Skill Descriptions
Occlumency 
The magical defence of the 
mind against external 
penetration, particularly from 
Legilimency (see page 44). 
A practitioner of Occlumency 
is an Occlumens. Occlumency 
is not a standard skill and will 
only be taught with special 
dispensation. Occlumency can 
help to resist the effects of the 
Veritaserum truth Potion. 
Persuasion 
Convincing others to see your 
point of view or to modify 
their decisions by use of 
persuasion. Can be through 
fast talk (Wits+Persuasion) 
or debate and oratory 
(Knowledge+Persuasion). 
Potions 
The practical brewing of 
magical potions. A delicate art 
involving rare ingredients, 
timing, ageing, stirring 
techniques, and careful 
bottling. Potions is a core class 
for the first five years. 
Prestige 
Your standing and influence 
in the wizard community. 
Prestige opens doors and calls 
in favours. Specialisations can 
include organisations like 
Hogwarts or the Ministry of 
Magic. Prestige is not always a 
positive thing. A strong 
connection with a particular 
organisation may make 
automatic enemies of anybody 
opposed to that group. 
Riding 
The use of beasts, magical and 
mundane) as mounts and their 
wrangling. Specialisations 
include specific beasts like 
Horses, Gryphons, and 
Thestrals, or even driving 
or piloting Muggle vehicles. 
Strategy 
The art of planning and 
analysis for tactical advantage. 
Useful for skirmishing (see 
below), Quidditch, Wizards 
Chess, and other sporting and 
martial pursuits. 
Skirmishing 
The art of brawling, swordplay, 
and disordered combat. Skill 
with close–combat and ranged 
weapons. Specialisations can 
include particular weapons or 
martial forms, and even 
different theatres of combat 
like forests, corridors, etc. 
Stealth 
Careful and silent movement 
that avoids detection. Stealth 
and Tracking is a course 
taught by the Auror’s Office. 
Specialisations can include 
particular environments, like 
urban or rural. 
Swimming 
Propelling yourself athletically 
through water for exercise, 
recreation, or competition. 
Specialisation include fresh or 
saltwater, diving, underwater 
swimming, or life saving. 
Tracking 
The ability to discover clues in 
your environment that reveal 
human and animal activity and 
to follow their movements. 
Stealth and Tracking is a 
course taught by the Auror’s 
Office. 
Transfiguration 
Study of the exacting and 
scientific art of 
Transfiguration. The alteration 
of the form or appearance of 
an object, animal or person. 
Transportation 
Study of the various methods 
of magical transportation, 
including Apparition, the Floo 
Network, and Portkey creation. 
Transportation Magic is 
heavily regulated by the 
Ministry of Magic and a 
licence is required. Practical 
lessons in Apparition are 
available from the age of 16, 
but you can only take your test 
when you reach the age of 17. 
Trivia 
You may have a particular area 
of interest, but you also have a 
magpie mind for all sorts of 
obscure facts and esoterica. 
Wandlore 
The mysterious, complex, and 
ancient lore of the wandmaker 
and the subtle laws of wands. 
Wandlore is covered in the 
sixth year of study.

characteristics; the wood that 
they are fashioned from, the 
length of the wand, the wand’s 
core, and the wand’s flexibility. 
You can choose the elements 
of your wand or roll them 
randomly using the tables on 
pages 25–26. Given that the 
wand chooses the witch or

accurate to roll randomly, but 
you may have something more 
specific in mind. 
Each type of wand wood and

bonus or bonuses (see pages

Buying Your Wand 
Most Hogwart’s students will 
have bought their wands from 
Ollivander’s in London at 
Diagon Alley’s South Side or 
from their branch on the High 
Street in Hogsmeade. Garrick 
Ollivander is certainly the 
most famous Wandmaker in 
Western Europe and arguably 
the most celebrated 
Wandmaker in the World. 
Ollivander only crafts wands 
using his three Supreme Cores; 
Unicorn hair, Dragon 
heartstring, and Phoenix 
feather. Ollivander wands cost 
7 Galleons each. 
In Eastern Europe, Mykew 
Gregorovitch is considered the 
finest Wandmaker. Wands can 
be purchased at his shop, 
Gregorovitch Zauberstäbe, 
somewhere in Central Europe, 
but are also available to buy at 
Wands by Gregorovitch sited 
in a Diagon Alley side street at 
the Carkitt Market Arcade. 
Prices upon request. 
Gregorovitch uses a wider 
range of wand cores, including 
Veela hair, Coral, and Thestral 
tail hair. 
Wand Loyalty 
When a wizard tries to use 
another Wizard’s wand, they 
normally have a +5 penalty to 
the difficulty of the spell (see 
page 41). Some wand woods 
and cores add a loyalty bonus 
to the +5 point penalty. Some 
characters have a rare gift 
called Wand Versatility (see 
page 10) that allows easier use 
of other wizard’s wands. 
Wand Resilience 
A wand, despite being a 
magically powerful object, is 
still made of wood and subject 
to breakage. Most wands have 
at least 2D10 resilience to 
resist damage, but more 
flexible wands will have a 
touch more yield (see page 26). 
In addition, particular wand 
woods offer greater resilience. 
See page 27–28 for details. 
Broken Wands 
The only known way to repair 
a wand is with the powerful 
and elusive Elder Wand. Short 
of locating that infamous 
instrument you can make a 
makeshift repair with 
Spellotape. 
Repaired wands are liable to 
misfire and add a +3 penalty 
to all spell difficulties. 
A failed spell roll with a 
broken wand will often cause 
an embarrassing backfire.
