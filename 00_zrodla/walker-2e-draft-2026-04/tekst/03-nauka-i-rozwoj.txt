Raising Attributes 
and Skills 
As your character matures, 
your attributes naturally 
increase with age. Each year 
(on your birthday), you can 
add one points to any one of 
your attributes. You can also 
increase one of you attributes 
at any time in exchange for a 
number of Story Points equal 
to five times the current level 
of the attribute. 
For example, raising an 
attribute from two to three 
costs ten Story Points (2 x 5). 
Increasing skills cost fewer 
Story points than attributes. 
Raising a skill by one point 
costs a number of Story Points 
equal to the current level of 
the skill plus three. 
For example, raising a skill 
from zero to one costs three 
Story Points (0+3). 
Learning Spells 
and Potions 
Learning spells and potions is 
recorded by marking off boxes 
on your spell sheet. The 
number in the leftmost box 
next to each spell or potion is 
the current difficulty target. 
Once all the boxes are filled in 
you’ve mastered that spell or 
potion and you no longer 
need to roll. 
You can fill in one box 
(working left to right) in 
exchange for one third of the 
number in the box, 
For example, the Unlocking 
Charm Alohomora: 
The beginning difficulty for 
Alohomora is 12, so you can 
fill in the first box for four 
Story Points. The difficulty 
then drops to nine, so for a 
further three points you can 
fill in the second box and 
master the spell. 
As well as spending Story 
Points to learn magic, you can 
make a roll to learn spells and 
potions through individual 
study (book learning), 
classroom study, or through 
one–to–one tuition (as when 
Professor Lupin tutored Harry 
to repel Dementors). Because 
classroom learning and one–
to–one tuition presents you 
with new knowledge and 
perspectives, you gain Story 
Points whenever you roll. 
Book Learning 
“And then, of course, there was 
the sheer size of the library; tens 
of thousands of books; thousands 
of shelves; hundreds of narrow 
rows. Hermione took out a list of 
subjects and titles she had 
decided to search while Ron 
strode off down a row of books 
and started pulling them off the 
shelves at random.” 
For spells and potions in your 
year, easily available textbooks 
like the Standard Book of 
Spells or Magical Drafts and 
Potions, will provide most of 
the information you need. 
For more advance or 
extracurricular Magic 
(particularly Dark Magic) you 
will need to visit the library. 
The Librarian will point you 
in the right direction, but 
you’ll need to do the hard 
work. Witches and Wizards are 
famously eccentric and fond 
of mysteries and cyphers and 
their books often mirror this. 
For every hour you spend 
searching through dusty old 
books you can roll 
1D+Knowledge+Archivist
with a target equal to the 
starting difficulty of the spell 
you are looking for. Getting a 
little help from your friends 
might be a good idea (see 
Working Together on page 6). 
Once you’ve located the spell 
or potion you can begin 
studying it. For every four 
hours of practice with the text 
to work from you can make a 
roll to learn the spell or 
potion.

Learning & Improving
Classroom 
The Core Subjects at 
Hogwart’s are allowed up to 
six hours of classroom time 
each week. Elective subjects 
like Alchemy and less formal 
settings like a Dueling Club, 
will generally have a three 
hour session. Ideally the 
Narrator won’t exhaustively 
play through every session on 
the timetable. Only the ones 
that add to the story. 
For every three hours of group 
work you can roll to learn or 
improve one spell or potion 
with a +1 bonus to your roll 
because of the support of your 
tutor and peers. If you roll the 
spell or potion’s current 
difficulty or above then you 
can mark off one box. You also 
gain a Story Point. 
If you fail your roll to learn 
the spell or potion then the 
Narrator will normally allow a 
second attempt during the 
week to reflect further 
sessions of that lesson in the 
timetable. Even if you fail both 
rolls, you can still attempt to 
perfect it through individual 
study or tuition (either from a 
helpful tutor or a talented 
fellow pupil). 
Tuition 
Once you’ve mastered a spell 
or potion, you can also 
instruct others. For every 
two hours of one–to–one 
instruction you can make a 
role to improve a spell. If your 
tutor’s magical skill (Charms, 
etc.) for that spell is above 
yours, then you can add +2 to 
your roll. 
As an example. Hermione had 
to quickly teach Harry Accio, 
the summoning charm, the 
day before his first task in the 
Tri–Wizard Tournament. They 
skipped lunch and carried on 
until 2am the next morning. 
Allowing for an Arithmancy 
class that Hermione insisted 
on attending and a hurried 
dinner, they had a little over 
ten hours for Harry to master 
the spell. Enough time for 
Harry to attempt five rolls (one 
per two hours). The spell has a 
starting difficulty of 12: 
To learn the spell, Harry had 
to first roll 12 or higher using 
1D+Focus+Charms+2. Having 
successfully rolled 12+, the 
difficulty drops to 9. 
To master the spell Harry only 
has to roll 9+ which he easily 
does with Hermione’s help. 
Having filled in both boxes, 
the spell difficulty becomes 
zero, so Harry is guaranteed to 
cast the spell when needed. 
The only problem is that 
Harry faces a Dragon for his 
first task and Dragons have a 
sizeable fear effect (see page 
89) which may leave Harry 
mentally Rattled or worse, 
with the resulting penalty 
adding to his target to cast the 
Summoning Charm. 
Faced with a Dragon, even the 
best prepared Wizard must 
tread carefully. 
Learning On 
The Hoof 
You can also learn a spell in 
the field. When you cast a spell 
you haven’t mastered in a 
critical situation (like Ron 
casting the Levitation Charm 
on a Troll’s club in the first 
year), you can mark off one 
box for that spell.
