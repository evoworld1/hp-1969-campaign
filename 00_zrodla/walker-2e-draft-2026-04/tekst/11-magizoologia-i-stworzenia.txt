Magizoology is the study of 
magical creatures and 
practitioners in this field are 
called Magizoologists. 
Students at Hogwarts are 
introduced to Magizoology 
through a third–year optional 
class called Care of Magical 
Creature. 
Most techniques for caring for 
magical creatures can be 
realised through a successful 
1D+Knowledge+Magical
Creatures roll (see below). 
Fieldwork 
A Magizoologist will 
frequently spend hours or 
even days tracking a magical 
creature: 1D+Wits+Tracking
Combined with an awareness 
of the most suitable habitats: 
1D+Knowledge+Observation. 
Once you’ve tracked down the 
magical creature or group of 
creatures, you may wish to 
observe them unseen. With 
the Erecto Charm or a little 
elbow grease, you can quickly 
construct a magically 
camouflaged hide: 
1D+Focus+Concealment. 
Add +3 to your roll if you cast 
Cave Inimicum (see page 49) or 
the Disillusionment Charm (see 
page 50), and +2 if you cast the 
Quietus Charm (see page 56). 
A roll of 12+ will hide you 
from any creatures in the 
vicinity while allowing you to 
observe freely. 
Useful spells (see pages 47–59) 
for Magizoologists include; 
Aguamenti to produce 
drinking water, Scourgify to 
cleans dirt from fur, scales, or 
feathers, Incarcerous for 
lassoing larger animals, the 
Imperturbable Charm to 
contain a feisty creature, 
Arresto Momentum, Immobulus, 
or Stupefy to slow or stop 
charging beasts, the Bubble 
Head Charm for dealing with 
underwater creatures, and 
Capacious Extremis for 
conveniently transporting 
animals.

Classification 
The Department for the 
Regulation and Control of Magical 
Creatures of the British Ministry of 
Magic classifies all known beasts, 
beings, and spirits, with a rating for 
the perceived danger level of each 
creature. There are five categories: 
XXXXX 
Known wizard killer. Impossible to 
train or domesticate. 
XXXX 
Dangerous. Requires specialist 
knowledge. Only skilled wizards 
should handle or approach these 
creatures. 
XXX 
Competent wizards should be able 
to manage these creatures. 
XX 
Harmless. Can be easily 
domesticated. 
X 
Boring. 
These ratings are highly subjective 
and reflect some of the prejudices 
of the Wizarding world. For 
example, Centaurs possess human 
intelligence and are highly 
civilised creatures. They are only 
classified as “beasts” at their own 
request, as they are unhappy to 
share “Being” status with hags, 
vampires, and other dark creatures. 
Despite all this they have a XXXX 
dangerous rating like many wild 
animals. Merpeople share the same 
rating, despite also being civilised 
intelligent creatures with their own 
evolved culture.

Magical Creatures 
The Ministry of Magic classify 
magical creatures as Beast, 
Being, or Spirit. 
A beast is a creature without 
sufficient intelligence to 
understand magical laws. 
While some extremists call for 
Muggles to be categorised as 
beasts, the category generally 
includes all non–sentient 
animalia. There are 
exceptions. Centaurs and 
Merpeople, despite their 
sentience, self–identify as 
Beasts as they refuse to be 
classified as Beings. A 
category that includes Hags 
and Vampires. 
A Being is any creature that 
has sufficient intelligence to 
understand the laws of the 
magical community and to 
bear part of the responsibility 
in shaping those laws (a 
definition that has stood since 
1811). There is a specialist 
Being Division within the 
Ministry’s Department for the 
Regulation and Control of 
Magical Creatures, which also 
houses the Liaison Offices for 
Goblins and Centaurs, and 
the Spirit Division. 
Spirit’s include ghosts (who 
complained that it was 
insensitive to classify them as 
Beings), and Non–Human 
Spiritous Apparitions (or 
Non–beings) like Banshees, 
Boggarts, Dementors, and 
Poltergeists. Non–Beings are 
generally Amortal. Having 
never been alive they cannot 
be killed, only banished. 
Descriptions 
Each creature description 
begins with their common 
Name and their Type (Beast, 
Being, or Spirit). 
Magical creatures have the 
same attributes as humans, 
but some may have zero in 
one or more. For example, 
beasts may lack Knowledge 
but still have Wits (instinct), 
incorporeal spirits may lack 
Vitality having no physical 
form but will still have powers 
that they can attack with. 
A creature without Vitality 
cannot be harmed physically, 
but may still be magically 
vulnerable. The Focus of an 
incorporeal creature takes the 
place of their Vitality for the 
purposes of magical attacks 
and damage. 
Some magical creatures, 
terrifying beasts and strange 
and otherworldly entities, 
also have a Fear rating. 
When you encounter such a 
creature roll 1D10+Wits. If 
you roll less than their Fear, 
check the difference on this 
table. The effect wears off one 
circle per round (see page 30). 
Creature Combat 
As well as the usual stats, each 
magical creature has a dice 
roll for their Move and 
Combat abilities. These are 
based on their Vitality plus 
Speed (Move) and their 
Vitality plus Wits (Combat). 
Roll their Move for initiative 
(see page 35) and pursuit (see 
page 37). Roll their Combat 
for Attack and Defence. 
These die rolls speed up 
creature combat and allow 
more opportunity for a 
character’s sheer good luck to 
prevail when fighting these 
fantastic beasts. 
Some creatures, like the 
Basilisk, Chimaera, and the 
Manticore are highly 
venomous (see page 38) and 
pose a very serious threat to 
even the most powerful witch 
or wizard. A Narrator who sets 
these deadly creatures on 
their players, must do so with 
great care and consider the 
availability of antidotes. The 
cure for Basilisk venom is well 
known, the tears of the 
Phoenix, but the others are 
unknown, allowing some 
leeway for the Narrator to 
insert imaginative remedies 
into their adventures.
